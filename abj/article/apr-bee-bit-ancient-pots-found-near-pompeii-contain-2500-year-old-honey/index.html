New Scientist Ancient pots found near Pompeii contain 2500-year-old honey The contents of an ancient Greek pot found in a shrine near Pompeii are testament to the longevity of a jar of honey. In 1954, a Greek burial shrine dating to around 520 BC was discovered in Paestum, Italy, about 70 kilometres south of Pompeii. There were eight pots in the shrine containing a sticky residue, whose identity has been a mystery ever since their discovery… Further analysis by Elisabete Pires, at the University of Oxford, revealed the presence of proteins called major royal jelly proteins, which are secreted by honeybees, as well as some peptides whose closest match was from Tropilaelaps mercedesae, a parasitic mite that feeds on the larvae of honeybees.
