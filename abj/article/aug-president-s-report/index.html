President's Report

Lindsay Callaway · Page 5 · August 2026

As this edition reaches members, many of Victoria's beekeepers would have placed their hives for another almond pollination season. For some, it could mean finding their first varroa. For most (as it is for my operation) it will mean a full year with varroa. For all, it will bring an increased likelihood of ‗catching' the new resistant mite.

For all beekeepers, it marks the beginning of another busy spring of swarm calls, queen rearing and honey production. We're simply doing what we love - caring for our bees.

Whatever your operation looks like, I wish you a safe and successful season. Please look after yourselves, your teams, your fellow beekeepers, and your bees. Good planning, clear communication and managing fatigue are just as important as having strong colonies.

The arrival of varroa has continued to bring beekeepers to work together, share knowledge and support one another. We become stronger because of our common adversary.

That spirit of collaboration continues to drive the work of your VAA.

One Voice - standing up for Victoria's beekeepers.

This month, VAA took its message directly to Victoria's minister for agriculture with a simple but important point: Victoria is facing a structural shift in beekeeping and pollination. Certainly not a temporary problem that will simply fix itself.

Our briefing paper outlined how varroa has fundamentally changed the economics of commercial beekeeping. The rising costs of treatment and monitoring, tighter operating margins, an ageing workforce, and increasing colony losses. These are no longer future risks; they are today's reality.

But the real message extends well beyond beekeeping.

As feral honeybee populations continue to decline, Victoria's beekeepers will undoubtedly feel the pressure in the short term. This season, we will need to ‗buckle up' for intensified reinfestation. In the longer term, Victoria's horticulture will also face increasing challenges. When our bees suffer, so does agriculture. They are two sides of the same coin.

Every year, honeybee pollination underpins billions of dollars of Victoria's agricultural production across almonds, pears, cherries, berries, vegetables and seed crops. The value of that contribution extends far beyond the honey industry itself, yet it is often taken for granted.

Victoria's food security depends on only a few hundred commercial beekeeping businesses. On average, each of those businesses support tens of millions of dollars of agricultural production. As varroa removes feral honey bee populations, the dependence on commercial beekeepers will only increase.

Government has demonstrated, through its investment in avian influenza preparedness, that it understands the value of acting before a crisis develops.

We are asking for that same leadership in pollination security.

The cost of waiting until pollination shortages become widespread, as they have already begun to emerge interstate, will be far greater than investing early in maintaining a strong, resilient beekeeping industry.

This is exactly why your VAA exists.

Our role is to be One Voice for Victoria's beekeepers - to advocate respectfully, provide practical solutions, engage constructively with government, growers and industry partners, and to ensure the contribution beekeepers make to our economy, environment and food security is properly recognised.

I am proud of the work the VAA team invested in preparing the briefing paper we took to the minister, and I'm even prouder that VAA will continue to speak confidently and constructively on behalf of Victoria's beekeepers.

Saying we are One Voice is easy.

Demonstrating it through action is what matters.

One Home - strengthening your VAA.

Advocacy is only one part of VAA's role.

Our greatest strength has always been our members.

VAA is much more than its board. It is our branches, affiliated associations, volunteers, mentors, and the countless members who generously give their time to support others.

And it is also the Tuesday night meeting in the community hall.

Supporting our branches and affiliated clubs.

One important piece of work currently underway is through our recreational subcommittee.

Its role is to listen carefully, strengthen relationships and make it easier for branches and affiliated clubs to access shared resources, education, guest speakers and training opportunities... rather than every club having to reinvent the wheel.

Over the coming months, the subcommittee will be contacting branches and affiliated organisations to better understand what is working well, where support is needed, and how VAA can better serve members across the state.

Our local clubs are the heart of Victoria's beekeeping. They always have been, and they always will be.

Building capability through EdComm.

Another project I am particularly proud of is our DEECA-funded Education and Communications (EdComm) program.

In just twelve months, a relatively small team of volunteers and contractors has achieved an extraordinary amount.

Together they have brought expertise from commercial, recreational and educational beekeeping, communications and extension. They have audited our existing educational material, identified six distinct beekeeper audiences to ensure future training is targeted and relevant, and developed the foundations of Victoria's first structured Varroa Integrated Pest Management curriculum.

Most importantly, they have taken that work directly to beekeepers.

This year, we delivered two full-day workshops. One in Swanpool with the North East Apiarists' Association for commercial and sideline beekeepers, and another in Cairnlea with the Moonee Ponds Beekeeping Association. Both were open to all beekeepers.

Together these workshops attracted almost 170 registrations and achieved an average participant satisfaction rating of 4.6 out of 5. To me, those numbers represent something much bigger than successful workshops. They represent beekeeper resilience. People are investing their own time to learn, adapt and prepare for the future.

Delivering all of this within budget, through the commitment of a relatively small team, is something everyone involved should be genuinely proud.

Building on the Australian Bee Journal.

One of the clearest outcomes of our EdComm work has been recognising just how important the Australian Bee Journal (ABJ) remains in connecting with Victoria's beekeepers.

For that reason, the board has established a new ABJ Editorial and Communications Working Group, which I am proud to be chairing. The board has also greenlighted a three-edition transition to reinvigorate the look and feel of ABJ to bring it in alignment with the new ‗One Home, One Voice, One Guardian VAA.

The group's role is to support our Biosecurity Editor, Kris Fricke, in shaping the Journal's editorial direction, while ensuring that quality information flows consistently across all our communication channels including member bulletins, HiveMeet, social media, VAA website and the soon-to-be-launched ABJ website.

The Working Group includes our Secretary Tino Corsetti, EdComm representative Andrew Wootton, and Olivia Ducommun-Dit-Verron representing our digital and social media communications.

Together we are guiding a thoughtful digital-first evolution of the Journal while respecting the publication that has served Victoria's beekeepers for generations.

Australian Bee Journal remains one of your VAA's greatest assets.

Our responsibility is to ensure it remains just as relevant and valuable for the next generation of beekeepers as it has been for the last.

Melbourne Royal Show update.

It is hard to believe another Melbourne Royal Show is almost upon us.

Once again, our dedicated volunteer committee is preparing to showcase the very best of Victoria's honey in the Gourmet Pantry Pavilion.

The Show remains one of VAA's most important fundraising activities while also giving us an invaluable opportunity to promote bees, pollination and premium Victorian honey to hundreds of thousands of visitors.

Thank you to the many honey producers who have already donated honey for this year's event.

Your generosity directly supports VAA's ability to deliver education, member services and industry programs throughout the year.

If you have surplus honey you would be willing to donate, please contact either myself, Richie Collins or VAA office. Every donor will be recognised through our promotional material during the Show and across our communication channels.

Later this month, we will also be calling for volunteers to help staff the stand. If you can spare a few hours, we would love to have you involved.

Growing Together

The VAA Recreational Subcommittee Gets to Work A new subcommittee, a clear purpose – connecting with and championing Victoria's local and regional bee clubs.

For as long as the Victorian Apiarists' Association has existed, its strength has come from the beekeeping communities around the state, typically centred around the local beekeeping club. The Tuesday-night meeting in a community hall. The mentor who turns up early to help a beginner light a smoker properly. The committee of volunteers quietly keeps an ancient hobby alive for the next generation. It is this network of clubs and branches, scattered across every corner of the state, that gives the VAA its reach and its relevance.

This year, the VAA has formed a new Recreational Subcommittee. Its mandate: build stronger connections between the VAA and its affiliated recreational clubs, listen more closely to what those clubs need, and find practical ways to support the volunteers who run them.

MEET THE TEAM

Chairing the new subcommittee is Stafford Kelly, of Lockwood South near Bendigo. Stafford grew up on a family farm at Inglewood, where beekeeping was simply part of daily life, before a career in the Australian Army that spanned senior roles in education, training and operational planning. Since returning to Bendigo, he has worked in commercial beekeeping and now chairs the VAA Bendigo Branch. Stafford brings a deep, practical understanding of what it takes to keep a regional club running – and a real passion for education, peer inclusion and building resilience across the industry. The Bendigo Branch is a thriving beekeeping club with a vibrant program of events, communications, mentoring and training.

Supporting the subcommittee in his role as VAA President is Lindsay Callaway, a fifth-generation apiarist. Lindsay's role on the subcommittee is as the liaison with the VAA board. Lindsay is also a member of the Bendigo Branch and contributes to many of its programs and events. Lindsay also enjoys travelling to speak at recreational bee clubs.
