Amitraz resistance (April 14 th ) Not surprisingly the announcement of miticide resistant bees got beekeepers talking, with both extensive discussions on the Central Victorian Apiarist Association page and Commercial beekeepers. In the latter Robert Seagrave offered more information on the origin of the hives he says were the first to show this resistance. beekeepers Why Can't We Be Like Canada? (April 22 nd ) The posting of an article from Bee Culture magazine about the approval of oxalic glycerin strips in Canada incited a discussion regarding why the APVMA hasn't done similar for us here. A particular comment by Nicholas Charters got numerous likes and “well said” responses so I'll reproduce it here in full:

Hi Rod, the beekeepers in the industry that do what they want with disregard for others [are what] we should all be concerned about. We acknowledge what has been done to date by AHBIC and the govt employees who have the industry at heart. Of course we need an approval authority to regulate chemical use in the Ag industry. However this discussion is not asking for own use of DDT or even synthetic chemicals! In Canada their equivalent of AHBIC obtained approval for own use OA methodology from their equivalent to APVMA. The question raised therefore asks can we in Australia seek to do the same. We have an industry that has been under sustained stress. The discussion comes from a posture of seeing good beekeepers lose hundreds of hives, profitability, businesses, relationships etc Given the evolving situation with mite resistance to Bayvarol and Apivar, and looking ahead at the approved treatment schedule it is pertinent to question does it adequately accommodate:  Sequential knock down options during spring and summer  Cost effective treatments  Year round honey boxes in subtropical climates (autumn bees currently wanting to be in triples not singles)  Rotating 4 approved treatments per year where resistance is present  We all want an industry that can:

 Reduce synthetic reliance with associated residue risks

 Use organic treatments wherever possible

 Sustain profitability and still produce premium table honey rather than transition to a pollination-only business model

 Maintain treatment compliance records

 legitimately sign off on annual Code compliance Those of us who have seen our colleagues suffer and fail whilst trying to work with current options have a responsibility to forewarn that there needs to be consideration of further changes for the industry to survive and prosper and we need our representative bodies to listen and back us.

AHBIC CEO Danny LeFeuvre's article “Miticide Registrations, APVMA and the Role of AHBIC“ which also appears in this same issue (page 31) is effectively the other side of this same discussion.

Unanswered Questions About Oxalic Acid (April 24 th ) A very spirited discussion about how Oxalic Acid actually works developed in response to the posting of a snippit of a Randy Oliver article on “Aussie Apiarists Online.” 23 people participated in this discussion putting forward numerous theories, though ultimately it is not conclusively known.
