Varroa Spread Map: August

Kris Fricke · Page 28 · September 2026

The alternative track is very steep and only a single lane so I was nervous that a car would be coming up the track. To make matters worse I had to shift the truck during loading and run over the smoker. Best to have a spare smoker.

Customers are ringing seeking advice about varroa. It seems there is a lot of unreported varroa findings. It seems the Otways are infected and parts of the Western District.

A beekeeping friend has had a bad experience. He made his own oxalic acid strips following a YouTube video and sadly he has killed most of his bee hives. I think the temperature was incorrect and formic acid was created which killed his queens. Possibly the oxalic acid from the hardware store was not pure. I recommend purchasing treatments from proven sources. As a result, his bees were unable to earn money from almond pollination, so back to shearing sheep.

Beekeepers need to be careful when using oxalic acid, it is dangerous as it will cause burns. I see on YouTube beekeepers handling strips without gloves or using leather gloves. You must use nitrile gloves with no slits or holes. Acid burns may not be obvious for some hours. Making your own oxalic acid treatments must be done with caution. A beekeeper showed me how traces of the oxalic acid on the mixing vessels must be cleaned up using bicarbonate soda and how utensils fizz as the bicarbonate soda neutralises the acid.

Beekeepers that have had varroa from the first infections are reporting that when the varroa numbers are about 16 per wash they “vape” the hive and then add oxalic acid strips. The strips seem to work for at least 8 weeks before the next treatment for which they are using Swedish cloths laid on top of the frames. It is important to have the oxalic acid strips near the centre of the brood nest for the warmth to do the job. The beekeeper thought the strips may have been exhausted after 8 weeks and the bees had chewed them down, so he touched his tongue with the strip and discovered they were still very much active [we want to be clear that you should under no conditions lick any miticide strips -Ed.]. I do not want to use vaping as I think it is dangerous especially with workers. The oxalic acid dribble seems safer way to treat the bees.

An elderly beekeeper told me that he is unable to cope with the coming varroa and the apiary officers wanting him to make honey samples and file Bee Max reports, so the remaining bee hives he has killed with petrol. Now has no more problems! Another beekeeper has told me that he cannot afford the cost of varroa treatments so is giving away his hives. He will concentrate on catching swarms for a fee.

A beekeeper from near Avoca discovered that his 80 bee hives were full of honey but the bees had dwindled away to small handfuls. Not doing anything to treat hives with varroa results in dead hives. He decided to retire and has sold his truck, loader, hives and spare equipment. Doing nothing, your bees will most likely die. Bees at the almond pollination that come into contact with it will find it is much quicker killing the bees. It requires immediate treatment.

Brazilian organic honey is being challenged by American beekeepers. Is it authentic? Argentine beekeepers have to provide tracing of their honey and its source through to extraction. Beekeepers have to apply for an electronic code which can be checked by police when they check truckloads of bees, honey or equipment. Export markets are demanding traceability.

20/8/2026 Hives that I have located on the edge of the Geelong suburb of Grovedale have wintered very poorly. Despite yellow gum and wild mustard flowering throughout the winter the bees have eaten their stores and are weak. Another bee site near St Albans Park have wintered a little better but also are light. I have been unable to open them due to the cool weather. I suggest if other hives in the Geelong suburbs are similar, they will need feeding.

23/8/2026 The first flowers are appearing on the Manchurian pears [Pyrus ussuriensis]. The Greater City of Geelong Council has planted many nature strips with these trees. They flower early spring and due to different varieties flower over a staggered time. They yield nectar and pollen. I suspect cause the bees to swarm due to copious brood making.

The new suburbs that are developing quickly do not have much room for gardens as the house occupies most of the ground. The gardens are generally minimal gardens so there is very little flowering plants for bees. The developers knock down the existing trees to make the building blocks, so it is only the new nature strip trees that need to grow to maturity. Seems wrong especially as the Greater Geelong City Council is urging people to plant trees as there is very little tree cover across the city.

28/8/2026 The farmer rang to say he needed to apply fungicide to the canola crop. Fortunately, most fungicides used for canola appear to be safe for bees. I will watch closely the result. Still no weight gains at this crop. In a remote area I inspected hives for varroa and all the double eight frame hives were clean. As I was about to leave, I inspected some overwintered five frame nucs and was astonished to discover varroa in low levels. [NSW beekeeper] Stephanie says that she thinks the presence of virgin queens in colonies attracts drones carrying the varroa.

Report from the almond pollination that the scale hive increased 11 kg in weight during the pollination. Barrel clover [Medicago truncatula] flowered very well, and some white mallee [Eucalyptus dumosa] flowered.

Varroa is increasing and hives located in the Inverleigh and Teesdale area are at risk of infection. I checked hives at two locations and found approximately 30% of the hives had varroa in the drone brood.

The spring is looking good with abundant capeweed [Arctotheca calendula] starting to flower. Clover species have flowers and the bottlebrushes are heavily budded with some starting to flower.
