Our role is to represent members and advocate for the protection and sustainable management of the floral resources on which Victorian beekeeping depends. Every region has a dedicated representative, and we encourage members to connect with them to share local issues, observations and ideas. The subcommittee is currently progressing a number of projects. We look forward to keeping members informed as this work develops and continuing to ensure that beekeepers' knowledge and experience contribute to decisions that affect our industry.

Following Through on DEECA Commitments

The subcommittee has reviewed outcomes from a workshop held in April 2026 between the Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA), Forest Fire Management Victoria (FFMV) and beekeeping industry representatives. The workshop repre-

sented an important step forward in rebuilding relationships between government agencies and the beekeeping industry. Commitments made during the workshop included improved internal education across government, a renewed focus on collaboration with industry stakeholders and a more constructive approach to public land access.

Our focus now is on ensuring those commitments translate into meaningful outcomes. To assist this process, we will continue working with DEECA and FFMV on their agreed actions and seek ongoing progress updates.

We have also been discussing the importance of helping government agencies better understand the role of beekeeping in Victoria. To support DEECA's internal education efforts, we will begin assembling photos, videos and supporting resources that showcase the work of Victorian beekeepers and the industry's contribution to regional communities and the environment. If you'd like to contribute to this project, please let us know.

We are working collaboratively with DEECA to codevelop a guide that brings together essential information in one place, including step-by-step instructions for using MapShare, applying for apiary sites, and accessing statewide contact lists for relevant government agencies and staff. The toolkit will also include a standardised public land bee site application form, guidance on planned burns and navigating notification systems, key contacts for bushfire and emergency support, and best-practice information on responsible apiary site use and working collaboratively with other forest users.

We aim to create a practical reference that improves access to information, reduces administrative complexity and helps beekeepers engage more effectively with land managers and government agencies.

In its conceptual phase at the moment, we are also exploring working with government mapping specialists to create a simplified version of Mapshare containing only the information relevant to apiculture. Such a tool has the potential to significantly reduce the administrative burden currently faced by beekeepers, with the hope that this increases accessibility to the public land bee site application process.

Resources Committee Seasonal Reports

The Resources Subcommittee will coordinate statewide seasonal reports for publication in the Australian Bee Journal. Drawing on reports from our re-

gional representatives, the publication will provide members with a snapshot of seasonal conditions across Victoria. We also intend to share this information with FFMV to better incorporate beekeepers' knowledge into planned burning discussions and avoidance of critical flowering periods when burn programs are being developed. Keep an eye out in future issues!

Looking to the Future

As we move forward, our focus remains on ensuring Victorian beekeepers have a strong voice in conversations about land management, resource access and the future of the industry. To strengthen our future advocacy efforts, the subcommittee will begin drafting formal resource policy positions that clearly articulate the industry's views on key land management issues.

We are keen to hear from members as this work progresses. The Resources Subcommittee exists to represent Victorian beekeepers, and the best outcomes are achieved when we draw on our collective knowledge and experience. If you have ideas, concerns or local issues that you believe deserve attention, please get in touch with your regional representative or a member of the subcommittee (see page 32). Your input helps shape our priorities and ensures our advocacy reflects the needs of beekeepers right across Victoria. We look forward to working with you as these projects continue to develop.
