A sting of hope for Parkinson’s disease

Scientists investigate bee venom as a potential companion to conventional Parkinson‘s therapy

CHINESE MEDICAL JOURNALS PUBLISHING HOUSE CO., LTD.

Parkinson‘s disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder caused by the loss of dopamine-producing neu-

rons, leading to motor symptoms such as tremors, rigidity, slowed movement, and impaired balance, as well as cog-

nitive decline. Although current treatments help relieve symptoms, they cannot stop disease progression.

Levodopa (L-DOPA), given with carbidopa, remains the standard treatment for PD by restoring dopamine levels and

improving motor function. However, its benefits often decline with long-term use, and complications such as motor

fluctuations and dyskinesias may develop. These limitations have prompted the search for adjunct therapies that

could improve treatment outcomes.

In this context, researchers from the University of Guadalajara investigated whether bee venom could improve the

therapeutic effects of L-DOPA/carbidopa in a mouse model of PD. Bee venom contains biologically active com-

pounds, including melittin, phospholipase A2, and apamin, which have demonstrated anti-inflammatory, antioxidant,

and neuroactive properties. ―We aimed to explore whether bee venom could potentiate the effects of standard thera-

py in a 6-hydroxydopamine (6-OHDA) model of Parkinson‟s disease,‖ explained lead author Professor Alma Karen

Lomeli-Lepe from the University of Guadalajara. The study was published in Neuroprotection on May 20, 2026.

Overall, the results suggest that bee venom may amplify the functional benefits of L-DOPA/carbidopa, improving

both motor and cognitive outcomes in experimental Parkinson‘s disease. ―Our findings demonstrate promising be-

havioral benefits of bee venom as an adjunct to L-DOPA/carbidopa therapy, but further research is needed to under-

stand the underlying mechanisms,‖ noted Prof. Lomeli-Lepe. While additional research is required, these findings

highlight a promising avenue for developing adjunct therapies aimed at improving outcomes for people living with

Parkinson‘s disease.

A very interesting article in the June 2026 American

Bee Journal by Randy Oliver the American scientist

beekeeper. He had been testing the pH levels of oxalic

acid treatments on the surface of bees after oxalic acid

treatments. He found anomalies in the experiment re-

sults. He concluded that it was caused by bee venom

being acidic. It was neutralizing the oxalic acid. Fur-

ther reviewing research papers revealed that worker

bees smear bee venom over themselves during self-

grooming. Bee venom is antimicrobial and contains

peptides and melittin. It was concluded that venom

bathing may be used by bees to reduce varroa infesta-

tion. It was found that bees that did not have varroa

had less venom on themselves. Queens and drones did

not have a coating of venom. The question is do Apis

cerana also use venom to protect themselves? There

are variations in varroa mite fertility, could this be due

to bee venom? I have found bee venom ointment to be

useful in reducing pain from arthritis.

My friend pollinates GMO seed crops near Hay

NSW. He says the GMO canola does not yield a honey

crop. Another friend pollinated the GMO canola seed

and said he was never going back as the bees came off

while walking along looking at the flowering canola a

monster-size king brown snake struck at his thigh, for-

tunately he stepped sideways and the snake missed as

they cannot move sideways once they start the strike.

Beware of standing behind a snake as they can spring

backwards the length of their body.

In the Brisbane Ranges a large colony of cockatoos

formed as the birds prune the trees. Under the flower-

ing trees the ground can be white with flowers.

The flowering yellow gums around my property are

full of hundreds of rainbow lorikeets [Trichoglossus

moluccanus]. They noisily screech to each other and

fly quickly to fresh trees. Their numbers seem to have

increased dramatically as 14 years ago my late wife

would feed them and the most that came to the feeder

was 36.
