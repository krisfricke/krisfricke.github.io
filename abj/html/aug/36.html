Imported honey

AHBIC‘s consistent work in this area over the past

four years has finally shown results. DAFF have been

very accommodating to work with, despite being pain-

fully slow! In late 2025 and early 2026 DAFF engaged

the states and territories food authorities to conduct a

spontaneous survey, to collect samples from importers.

Under the current protocol in the Imported Food In-

spection Scheme (IFIS), all samples passed for adulter-

ation. However, when the same samples were tested

via NMR testing, a significant problem was discovered.

This clearly demonstrates that the current protocol is

insufficient. DAFF are currently investigating the next

course of action. The objective for AHBIC is to have

the IFIS protocol adjusted to a modern-day method.

This would be a huge win for the Australian beekeeping

industry.

The roll of volunteers

I would like to take the opportunity to thank all the

people who volunteer their time for the benefit of the

industry. There are likely over a hundred people who

contribute to an organization in one way or another.

Whether it be cooking the barbeque at your local

branch, working at shows, sitting on committees or ex-

ecutives, it all helps and contributes to the common

goal to better our industry.

It is important to be supportive of each other and

encourage people to step up and contribute to these

roles. Social media highlights everything that we do, it

is important not to criticize individuals online, particu-

larly for holding a position where they are volunteering.

For the thick skinned, it may be water off a ducks back,

however the next generation are watching. In the back

of their minds, they may be hesitant to step up observ-

ing this criticism. Think about what you say, is it nec-

essary or helpful?

Finally, the industry was extremely saddened by the

loss of James Kershaw, a great mate, a great person and

Australian Honey Bee Industry Council Annual

General Meeting

On Sunday, 12 July, AHBIC held its AGM at the

Launceston Country Club. The AGM attracted 25 ob-

servers and 20 representatives from around the country.

In addition to the standard procedural content, delegates

heard from Dr John Roberts from CSIRO about De-

formed Wing Virus (DWV) and the ability of drone

semen and imported honey to carry viable DWV. Dele-

gates passed a series of motions for AHBIC to lobby

the federal government to ensure the risks are appropri-

ately managed.

Participants also heard from ICS Consulting, who

recently completed a traceability feasibility project for

the federal government through AgriFutures. This pro-

ject highlighted the possibility of a national hive tracea-

bility program that could track hives for biosecurity and

honey traceability purposes. Delegates gained insights

into the proposed structure and design of the potential

system. They also heard about the strong government

interest in the system, while gaining a clear understand-

ing of the many barriers that would need to be over-

come.

Chair and executive elections were held with Jon

Lockwood being re-appointed for a two-year term

along with Dr Cooper Schouten also being re-appointed

for a second two-year term. We welcomed Mick Palm-

er from Tasmania who was voted in to replace the retir-

ing Dr Ben McKee who has served his maximum 3

continuous terms.

I would like to acknowledge Dr Ben McKee‘s ten-

ure on AHBIC. Not only has Ben served three terms

as a director he has also served as Chair of AHBIC‘s

Food Safety Committee and as a delegate for the Honey

Packers and Marketers Association for many years.
