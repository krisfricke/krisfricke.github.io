Australian Bee Journal

President’s Report, cont’d

The experienced beekeeper helping someone light a

smoker for the very first time. The volunteer commit-

tee organising another field day. The branch secretary

quietly keeping everything running behind the scenes.

That is One Home in action.

Supporting our branches and affiliated clubs.

One important piece of work currently underway is

through our recreational subcommittee.

Its role is to listen carefully, strengthen relationships

and make it easier for branches and affiliated clubs to

access shared resources, education, guest speakers and

training opportunities... rather than every club having to

reinvent the wheel.

Over the coming months, the subcommittee will be

contacting branches and affiliated organisations to bet-

ter understand what is working well, where support is

needed, and how VAA can better serve members across

the state.

Our local clubs are the heart of Victoria‘s beekeep-

ing. They always have been, and they always will be.

Building capability through EdComm.

Another project I am particularly proud of is our

DEECA-funded

Education

and

Communications

(EdComm) program.

In just twelve months, a relatively small team of

volunteers and contractors has achieved an extraordi-

nary amount.

Together they have brought expertise from commer-

cial, recreational and educational beekeeping, commu-

nications and extension. They have audited our exist-

ing educational material, identified six distinct bee-

keeper audiences to ensure future training is targeted

and relevant, and developed the foundations of Victo-

ria's first structured Varroa Integrated Pest Manage-

ment curriculum.

Most importantly, they have taken that work directly

to beekeepers.

This year, we delivered two full-day workshops.

One in Swanpool with the North East Apiarists' Associ-

ation for commercial and sideline beekeepers, and an-

other in Cairnlea with the Moonee Ponds Beekeeping

Association. Both were open to all beekeepers.

Together these workshops attracted almost 170 reg-

istrations and achieved an average participant satisfac-

tion rating of 4.6 out of 5. To me, those numbers repre-

sent something much bigger than successful work-

shops. They represent beekeeper resilience. People are

investing their own time to learn, adapt and prepare for

the future.

Delivering all of this within budget, through the

commitment of a relatively small team, is something

everyone involved should be genuinely proud.

Building on the Australian Bee Journal.

One of the clearest outcomes of our EdComm work

has been recognising just how important the Australian

Bee Journal (ABJ) remains in connecting with Victo-

ria‘s beekeepers.

For that reason, the board has established a new

ABJ Editorial and Communications Working Group,

which I am proud to be chairing. The board has also

greenlighted a three-edition transition to reinvigorate

the look and feel of ABJ to bring it in alignment with

the new ‗One Home, One Voice, One Guardian VAA.

The group's role is to support our Biosecurity Edi-

tor, Kris Fricke, in shaping the Journal's editorial direc-

tion, while ensuring that quality information flows con-

sistently across all our communication channels includ-

ing member bulletins, HiveMeet, social media, VAA

website and the soon-to-be-launched ABJ website.

The Working Group includes our Secretary Tino

Corsetti, EdComm representative Andrew Wootton,

and Olivia Ducommun-Dit-Verron representing our

digital and social media communications.

Together we are guiding a thoughtful digital-first

evolution of the Journal while respecting the publica-

tion that has served Victoria‘s beekeepers for genera-

tions.

Australian Bee Journal remains one of your

VAA’s greatest assets.

Our responsibility is to ensure it remains just as rele-

vant and valuable for the next generation of beekeepers

as it has been for the last.

Melbourne Royal Show update.

It is hard to believe another Melbourne Royal Show

is almost upon us.

Once again, our dedicated volunteer committee is

preparing to showcase the very best of Victoria‘s honey

in the Gourmet Pantry Pavilion.

The Show remains one of VAA‘s most important

fundraising activities while also giving us an invaluable

opportunity to promote bees, pollination and premium

Victorian honey to hundreds of thousands of visitors.

Thank you to the many honey producers who

have already donated honey for this year's event.

Your generosity directly supports VAA‘s ability to

deliver education, member services and industry pro-

grams throughout the year.

If you have surplus honey you would be willing to

donate, please contact either myself, Richie Collins or

VAA office. Every donor will be recognised through

our promotional material during the Show and across

our communication channels.

Later this month, we will also be calling for volun-

teers to help staff the stand. If you can spare a few

hours, we would love to have you involved.
