Also recognise the practical and compliance challenges that can arise where the available pack size does not align

In their email reply they

appreciate that this may result in increased product wastage and create difficulties for beekeepers seeking to

comply with the permit conditions. However, the APVMA can only assess and approve the information put before

us in an application. The pack size and associated label instructions under PER95790 were proposed by the per-

mit holder as part of their application. The APVMA cannot require a permit holder to market or supply alterna-

tive pack sizes.

The APVMA also suggested

The most effective way to pursue a change is for beekeepers and industry groups to raise the issue directly with

the permit holder. If the permit holder elects to seek approval for additional or smaller pack sizes, they may sub-

mit an application to the APVMA, and we would assess that application on its merits

What has become clear is that: AgVic, APVMA, VAA, Aluen CAP, and other clubs recognise the issue. No

single organisation can fix it immediately. The real opportunity for change will come after full registration, when

label amendments become more feasible. In the meantime, clubs will need to support members through coordina-

tion, education, and practical compliance advice.

As a club, we are strongly encouraging members to use their members zones, these are effectually six geograph-

ical areas around Geelong loosely based on Northern, Southern, Eastern and Western suburbs. Members within the

club can opt in to allow members in their zones to connect. The more connected each zone is, the easier it becomes

to coordinate treatments when they‘re needed.

Why I’m continuing to push

Because if we don‘t raise these issues, they remain invisible. Hobbyist beekeepers make up the majority of Victo-

ria‘s beekeeping community. We are part of the state‘s biosecurity system, and our ability to treat effectively matters

just as much as that of commercial operators. If treatment pack sizes are impractical for most beekeepers, then the

system isn‘t working as intended.

My role or should I say our role, as beekeepers, is to make sure that our message is heard clearly, respectfully,

and persistently.

What can you do? Please let your local member know that this remains a major concern for your club and your

members. I strongly encourage every beekeeper to contact their local federal and state members of parliament to out-

line the value of hobbyist beekeeping, and the real impacts varroa is having on your beekeeping.

Thank you to everyone who has shared their stories and supported this work. It‘s your lived experiences that give

these discussions genuine weight.

Nathan Whitford

President, Geelong Beekeepers Club
