As this edition reaches members, many of Victoria‘s

beekeepers would have placed their hives for another

almond pollination season. For some, it could mean

finding their first varroa. For most (as it is for my oper-

ation) it will mean a full year with varroa. For all, it

will bring an increased likelihood of ‗catching‘ the new

resistant mite.

For all beekeepers, it marks the beginning of another

busy spring of swarm calls, queen rearing and honey

production. We‘re simply doing what we love - caring

for our bees.

Whatever your operation looks like, I wish you a

safe and successful season. Please look after your-

selves, your teams, your fellow beekeepers, and your

bees. Good planning, clear communication and manag-

ing fatigue are just as important as having strong colo-

nies.

The arrival of varroa has continued to bring bee-

keepers to work together, share knowledge and support

one another. We become stronger because of our com-

mon adversary.

That spirit of collaboration continues to drive the

work of your VAA.

One Voice - standing up for Victoria’s beekeepers.

This month, VAA took its message directly to Victo-

ria‘s minister for agriculture with a simple but im-

portant point: Victoria is facing a structural shift in bee-

keeping and pollination. Certainly not a temporary

problem that will simply fix itself.

Our briefing paper outlined how varroa has funda-

mentally changed the economics of commercial bee-

keeping. The rising costs of treatment and monitoring,

tighter operating margins, an ageing workforce, and

increasing colony losses. These are no longer future

risks; they are today's reality.

But the real message extends well beyond beekeep-

ing.

As feral honeybee populations continue to decline,

Victoria‘s beekeepers will undoubtedly feel the pressure

in the short term. This season, we will need to ‗buckle

up‘ for intensified reinfestation. In the longer term,

Victoria‘s horticulture will also face increasing chal-

lenges. When our bees suffer, so does agriculture.

They are two sides of the same coin.

Every year, honeybee pollination underpins billions

of dollars of Victoria‘s agricultural production across

almonds, pears, cherries, berries, vegetables and seed

crops. The value of that contribution extends far be-

yond the honey industry itself, yet it is often taken for

granted.

Victoria‘s food security depends on only a few hun-

dred commercial beekeeping businesses. On average,

each of those businesses support tens of millions of dol-

lars of agricultural production. As varroa removes feral

honey bee populations, the dependence on commercial

beekeepers will only increase.

Government has demonstrated, through its invest-

ment in avian influenza preparedness, that it under-

stands the value of acting before a crisis develops.

We are asking for that same leadership in polli-

nation security.

The cost of waiting until pollination shortages be-

come widespread, as they have already begun to emerge

interstate, will be far greater than investing early in

maintaining a strong, resilient beekeeping industry.

This is exactly why your VAA exists.

Our role is to be One Voice for Victoria‘s beekeep-

ers - to advocate respectfully, provide practical solu-

tions, engage constructively with government, growers

and industry partners, and to ensure the contribution

beekeepers make to our economy, environment and

food security is properly recognised.

I am proud of the work the VAA team invested in

preparing the briefing paper we took to the minister,

and I‘m even prouder that VAA will continue to speak

confidently and constructively on behalf of Victoria‘s

beekeepers.

Saying we are One Voice is easy.

Demonstrating it through action is what matters.

One Home - strengthening your VAA.

Advocacy is only one part of VAA's role.

Our greatest strength has always been our members.

VAA is much more than its board. It is our branch-

es, affiliated associations, volunteers, mentors, and the

countless members who generously give their time to

support others.

And it is also the Tuesday night meeting in the com-

munity hall.
