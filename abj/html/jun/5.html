President’s Report

June 2026

5

Dear Victoria‘s beekeepers.

It‘s your industry.

It‘s your VAA.

And VAA is your collective voice and

peak body.

Beekeepers want the same things...

• We want healthy bees.

• We want viable businesses.

• We want practical solutions.

• We want to win the fight against var-

roa.

• We want a future worth handing to

the next generation.

However, for too long, parts of our industry have

been distracted by division and fragmented thinking.

Having and sharing different points of view is essential,

and it is necessary that we each bring our views to the

table. The future we build to adapt to the paradigm

shift of varroa will succeed only if we build that future

together.

After more than twelve months in this role, I have

enormous respect for every VAA president who has

come before me. Your Victorian Apiarists‘ Association

is relatively small in structure, yet massive in im-

portance to agriculture, food security and the future of

apiculture in this state. And yet, despite the challenges,

I have never been more optimistic.

Adversity has a way of revealing what matters. Var-

roa has now established itself across Victoria. Fuel

costs, seasonal pressure, increasing operating costs and

regulatory complexity continue to test commercial and

recreational beekeepers alike. The emergence of re-

sistant mite strains only sharpens that challenge further.

But I believe this challenge will ultimately make us

stronger.

Varroa is forcing us to evolve. It is forcing us to

collaborate. It is forcing us toward smarter, safer and

more sustainable management systems. And in many

ways, this transition will strengthen Victoria‘s beekeep-

ing for generations to come.

VAA exists to ensure that the transition ahead hap-

pens with Victoria‘s beekeepers at the centre of every

decision. By putting beekeepers first, VAA provides a

home for all Victoria‘s beekeepers. By putting bee-

keepers first, VAA provides a voice for all Victoria‘s

beekeepers. This has been a year of delivery, progress

and strategic momentum for Victoria‘s beekeeping.

Over the past twelve months, your VAA has deliv-

ered practical outcomes across education, advocacy,

research, industry support and association growth.

Thanks to a lot of work by a lot of people, here are

some key results/highlights…

Strategic direction and renewal

• Established the framework for the VAA Strategic

Plan.

• Formal articulation of VAA principles of One

Home. One Voice. One Guardian.

• Clearer alignment of advocacy, education and

member service priorities.

Biosecurity and education

• Delivery of the Advanced Com-

mercial

Varroa

Workshop

at

Swanpool.

• Delivery of the Specialist Varroa

Workshop at Cairnlea.

• Continued growth of HiveMeet as

Victoria‘s monthly digital learning

platform.

• Expansion of the VAA‘s online

education and recorded resource

library.

Industry capability and support

• Launch of the Apiary Business

Navigator Program.

• Development of the Peer-to-Peer Beekeeper Sup-

port framework.

• Practical support for Victorian beekeepers navi-

gating varroa transition.

Advocacy and policy

• Initiated and led the landmark DEECA Strategic

Roadmap Workshop.

• Progress through the Ag-Chem Working Group

toward practical treatment reform.

• Continued advocacy on public land access, pre-

scribed burning and beekeeper service fees.

• Stronger representation of honey bee food security

issues to government and agriculture.

Research and Long-term stewardship

• Progression of the Box Ironbark Forest research

grant.

• Strengthening of the Resources subcommittee.

• Development of practical long-term floral resource

stewardship pathways.

Community and association strength

• Record momentum leading into the 2026 Annual

Conference.

• Strong sponsorship support and growing industry

backing.

• Outstanding Melbourne Royal Show volunteer

contribution.

• Continued branch engagement and regional leader-

ship growth.

These are not isolated activities. They are strategic

building blocks. Together, they represent a VAA with

purpose and momentum.

One home. VAA continues to strengthen its role as

the home for all Victoria‘s beekeepers.

Across branches, workshops, digital education and

volunteer programs, we are seeing stronger engagement

from recreational, sideliner and commercial beekeepers

alike.

Importantly, we are seeing younger beekeepers step-

ping forward. This matters. Because the long-term

strength of our association depends on renewal.
