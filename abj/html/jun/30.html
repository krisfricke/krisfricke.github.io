Yellow legged hornets are becoming a big problem

in some countries overseas.

France, Spain and now Britain are unable to stop the

spread. Auckland, New Zealand, discovered an incursion

and are busy implementing high tech methods to try and

An article from The Beekeepers Quarterly (BKQ)

March 2026 explores the storage of pollen in the hive and

the part ―the art of lactic fermentation‖ plays. Briefly,

fungicides from crop sprays will destroy yeast cells

which lead to the spoilage of the bee bread. Formic acid

is often used as a miticide to treat varroa and the acid

disrupts the pH conditions and affects some of the bacte-

rial and yeast species. This presents another thing for us

to consider when treating varroa.

Another very interesting article in the same issue of

BKQ was about Tongan beekeeping. A summary of it:

all synthetic and organic treatments of varroa are banned

by the Tongan government. The tropical climate is very

humid which suppresses the varroa. This results in a re-

duction of varroa reproduction. Consequently, the bees

swarm all through the year. The Tongan bees have

adapted by using swarming to reduce the effects of var-

roa. When a hive swarms 60 to 70% of the varroa leave

the hive with the swarm. A broodless period occurs

while the queen is mating. This also occurs with the

swarm. It has been found that bees kept in small hives

are more productive. The recommended hive size is a ¾

size (WSP) brood nest and same size honey super over a

queen excluder. Some swarms will be weak and die. An

experiment by Loftus et al (2016)1 of 12 small hive and

12 large hives resulted in just one third of varroa numbers

in the small hives. Of the large hives only two survived

of the original 12. The smaller colonies are used mainly

for pollination of crops, and do not make much honey.

Large colonies produce large amounts of honey.

I find this fascinating as we are told that without

treatments the hives will die. Perhaps beekeepers need to

move away from the yellow Italian bees that make large

amounts of brood. More selection of Carniolan queens

that make small brood nests, yet can be as productive as

hives with large amounts of brood.
