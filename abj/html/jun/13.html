Chameleon’ bees change color with the weather

Queen Mary University of London

Some bees really do change colour with the weather, according to new research that shows humidity can temporarily

alter the shimmering hues of certain species. In a study published in Biology Letters, scientists led by Dr Madeleine

Ostwald of Queen Mary University of London found that moisture in the air can cause sweat bees to change colour

- and then change back again when conditions dry out. Sweat bees are known for their bright, metallic greens and

blues. Until now, reports that their colours could shift have been anecdotal. This new research provides the first

practice when it has worked under several different

conditions, from multiple keepers, with consistent posi-

takes time - seasons rather than weeks - but it ensures

that diversity of experience has had space to surface and

be weighed. The ―quorum‖ approach balances the need

for timely decisions (we cannot wait forever when colo-

nies are under pressure) with the need to avoid prema-

ture consensus built on thin evidence.

Seen this way, the study of honey‑bee group deci-

sion making becomes a mirror for human practice in the

apiary. To cultivate genuine ―beekeeper intelligence,‖

groups can adopt three hive‑inspired habits:

1. Structure discussions as open competitions of ide-

as rather than battles of ego, where unconventional ob-

servations are welcomed and tested rather than dis-

missed.

2. Actively promote diversity of knowledge and in-

dependence of opinion - different climates, hive types,

forage strategies, treatment philosophies, and scales of

operation all have something valuable to add.

3. Aggregate experience using transparent, quor-

um‑like thresholds that respect both time constraints

and the breadth of field knowledge, so that the practices

we converge on are robust, repeatable, and most im-

portantly, aligned with the bees‘ own wellbeing.
