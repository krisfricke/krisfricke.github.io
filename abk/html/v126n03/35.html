growing crops as efficiently as possible.

Now, keeping those realities of modern agriculture in

THE CROPS USED IN VEGAN HONEY

The most common ingredients I found in vegan honey

were sucrose, glucose, and fructose. All of these sugars

came either directly or indirectly from agronomic crops.

Sucrose comes from either sugar cane or sugar beets,

while glucose is made by hydrolyzing starch (usually

corn starch) with enzymes (particularly alpha-amylase

from bacteria). The next step, making fructose, is

performed by isomerizing glucose. However, sometimes

manufacturers substitute sorghum, wheat, or tapioca

(cassava) starches, for the cornstarch. Each of these

plants is also an agronomic crop.

In other words, the number of animals, including bees,

killed during the production of the sugar used to make

vegan honey is astronomical. But let’s say that instead of

the usual syrups, you base your bee-free honey on apple

juice concentrate, as some do. That may sound like an

answer, but if you think apple concentrate is bee-free,

think again: Countless honey bees worked their tails off

to pollinate the apples that go into the concentrate. In

truth, apple concentrate is about as bee-free as almond

milk.

Next, we have the botanical extracts, such as lemons,

also pollinated by honey bees. Agave gets pollinated

by bats primarily, red clover by bees other than honey

bees, buckwheat by honey bees, and dates by hand.

Flower flies and butterflies pollinate the chamomile, the

wind takes care of the seaberry, butterflies and bees

pollinate jasmine, while honey bees and carpenter bees

service the passionflowers. It seems you just can’t make

vegan honey without “exploiting” some kind of animal,

especially bees

A HEALTH CONSIDERATION

Unrelated to veganism, but noteworthy in any discussion

of honey substitutes, is their status as ultra-processed foods

(UPF). By definition, any food that contains an ultra-processed

ingredient is a UPF. According to recent research, UPF may be a

contributor to a panoply of health problems, including obesity,

cancer, type II diabetes, and heart disease.

Some foods have extremely high amounts of these ingredients,

while others have less. If you are trying to reduce your intake of

UPF, as many people are, vegan honey may be an item to cut.
