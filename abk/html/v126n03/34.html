THE PERILS OF FIELD CROPS

As an agronomist, I can attest that any production

field is a landscape of death and destruction for any

animal that gets in the way. Even discounting deaths

from chemical fertilizers and pesticides, each pass

of heavy equipment kills uncountable living things.

Plowing, discing, harrowing, planting, cultivating,

and harvesting all take a toll on a substance — soil —

that is normally teeming with life. Those machines

slice, stab, cut, crush, churn, and macerate the soil

and everything within it.

Online, you can find many field counts of death by

farm machinery. Sadly, those I found restricted their

counts to large, easy-to-count species such as deer,

rabbits, rats, field mice, and sometimes birds and

snakes. It’s clear that some people think of “mammal”

when they hear the word “animal,” and the small, the

slimy, and the slithery may be perceived as gross or

unworthy of counting. The problem reminds me of a

puzzling query that frequently arrives on my website:

“Is a bee an insect or an animal?”

Looking at the big picture, the victims in cultivated

fields also include turtles, frogs, moles, voles, snakes,

moths, butterflies, reptiles, worms, beetles, centipedes,

and millipedes. Oh yes, don’t forget the gophers,

groundhogs, ants, termites, wasps, caterpillars, flies,

maggots, spiders, and scorpions, along with crickets,

cockroaches, nematodes, mites, snails, and hundreds

more. Each plays a vital role in our ecosystems.
