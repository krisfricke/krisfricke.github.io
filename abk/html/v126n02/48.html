pupa, so generally would need no help from a beekeeper. So

the “proof of benefit” is really up to those selling a product.

I ran these experiments as an example of the sort of hard

data that we beekeepers would like to see, so that we can

make informed decisions as to how we spend our money.

The results of this particular study are for a single trial in

the California foothills (objective, impartial, blinded, and

replicated). However, similar results have been found by

other research groups (pers comm), including one,15 that

noted: “Probiotics, in theory and concept, are a promising

solution to enhance bee health, but the current market

available products for beekeepers are making claims that far

outreach the ability of their products.”
