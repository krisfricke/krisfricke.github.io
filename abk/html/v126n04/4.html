When I came across something about Ben Moore’s

honey vending machines this past month, I knew not

only was it an interesting story worth sharing with

everyone, but it was the perfect tie in to yet another

classic article I’d been wanting republish. In this case

I didn’t remember the author’s name and could only

vaguely describe it and the half decade range in which I

thought I’d read it to the American Bee Journal’s editor,

but as luck would have it he himself had only just come

back across it the prior week and knew exactly what I

was talking about. And so I’m pleased to bring you from

2018 a notably memorable article entitled “who steals

from a honey stand” in which one of the researchers

at the world famous Cornell bee lab applies scientific

methods to create a slightly tongue-in-cheek study of

theft from their honey stand. I think there are some

real insights there for anyone running an honour-system

honey stand.

Other than that we have a variety of interesting things

for you. CSIRO’s John Roberts wrote about Deformed

Wing Virus a much discussed factor in varroa impact

worldwide. Randy Oliver wrote about robbing screens

being used to slow varroa spread. Mike Allerton wrote

about a heat treatment to kill varroa that is already

available here in Australia for chemical free varroa

control. “How are we going to dispose of all this

miticide contaminated brood comb in the city?” is a

question that I’ve heard a lot, so when I came across

A cold, wet bee.

Mitcham, Vic

Ashton Edgley

an article about a club doing a used hiveware disposal

initiative in Auckland I knew this was something worth

sharing. Alan Wade gives us the second half of his

round up of general treatment strategies, and then

I was going to continue my planned varroa series but

realised we already had more than enough about varroa

for one issue and turned my attention to getting you

some interesting non-varroa topics:

Eleanor Schumacher had written an extremely good

article all about queens so we have that for you, as

well as David Burns writing about queen excluders and

isolators. It was one of my goals to bring more content

relating to equipment so I’m pleased to have another

how-to-build guide from Ed Simon, a honey super lifter

this time.

Parliament’s beekeeper Cormac Farrell has a new book

coming out. He was good enough to write an article

which should give you a pretty good idea what the book

is about – really multifaceted look at the value of urban

beekeeping and ways to really get into it. His book is

excellent and so is the article.

And finally, finally, because I like to pair things up,

after a letter-to-the-editor about rhubarb I couldn’t

resist a light-hearted anecdotal story by Ed Colby that

prominently features a rhubarb pie.

And so I think you’ll agree that there’s a nice mix of

different pertinent topics in beekeeper here which I’m

sure you’ll enjoy!
