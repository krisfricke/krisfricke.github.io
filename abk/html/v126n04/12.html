The last Saturday of August marked the inaugural Used

Hiveware Collection Day in Auckland, set against the backdrop

of a massive thunderstorm. While the weather may have

deterred some, our dedicated volunteers were undaunted

showing up to collect and safely dispose of about 50 bags of

old hiveware dropped off at the collection stations.

Importantly, we want to clarify that we had no reason

to believe any of the hiveware collected was infected with

AFB (American foulbrood), although there is always a non-

zero chance of subclinical AFB presence, where symptoms

haven’t yet manifested.

At Auckland Beekeepers Club, we fully understand the

risks of AFB transmission from used hiveware and the need

for ongoing education. With the help of local commercial

beekeepers and advice from Dwayne Hill of the AFB

Management Agency, we formulated a solid plan for the

day. Thanks to Ibrahim from Beezthingz, who generously

provided a burn pit, we were able to proceed with safe

disposal. Most volunteers hailed from the Bee Club, along

with some of Ken’s apiculture students just beginning their

beekeeping journey.

While the likelihood of AFB contamination in used hiveware

is low, it’s still a possibility, and reducing contamination

avenues, no matter how small, is critical. A significant part of

this initiative is raising awareness about AFB transmission risks.

Even the most skilled beekeepers face the ongoing threat of

AFB, and the industry must continue to mitigate these risks.
