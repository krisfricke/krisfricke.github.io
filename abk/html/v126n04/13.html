such as quarantining, trusting the source, and contacting

the AFB Management Agency for advice are essential.

What we did

We set up five collection hubs across Auckland—in North

Shore, Northwest, Central, East Central, and South. Each

team, equipped with a trailer, communicated via WhatsApp,

and our burn site was located south of the city. We asked for

a nominal donation per box or bin bag to cover costs, and

all volunteers, who even brought their own supplies, kindly

refused petrol money. Flexibility was key, with volunteers

offering discounts or waiving fees for those in need.

Why we did it

In Auckland, with many hobbyist beekeepers in urban

gardens, safely disposing of hiveware—especially plastic

frames—is more complicated. Used hiveware, even if not

linked to AFB, can attract bees and increase the risk of

disease transmission. Less-experienced beekeepers may also

misdiagnose AFB, further complicating efforts to control the

disease.

Is it necessary?

AFB spores can survive in hiveware for decades, meaning

even long-abandoned equipment may pose a risk. The

subclinical presence of AFB in seemingly healthy hives is

another factor, underscoring the complexity of managing

hive health.

Where do we go from here?

The Auckland Beekeepers Club plans to repeat the event

next year, exploring whether autumn, spring, or both seasons

would be ideal. We hope other clubs and commercial

operations will join this initiative.

What we’ll do differently next time

Two key areas for improvement: better promotion and

avoiding holding the event during a thunderstorm! The

initiative was well received by those involved, and we relied

primarily on social media and word of mouth for promotion.

Going forward, we’ll reconsider whether to reach out more

broadly, even to community groups that may include people

no longer keeping bees but with hiveware to dispose of.

What went well

Everyone involved was incredibly supportive, and many

wondered why such an initiative hadn’t been done earlier.

This simple, low-cost effort to safeguard bee health felt like a

crystallising moment, where individual action has the power

to create lasting industry change. All hiveware was brought

in sealed bin bags, and no one questioned the nominal

donation per box/bag, showing a shared understanding of

the initiative’s importance.
