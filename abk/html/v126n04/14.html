Unlike researchers working with E. coli, fruit flies, or bats,

we honey bee researchers get a valuable, if unintentional,

byproduct: honey. The Liddell Lab at Cornell University is

certainly not in the honey business, but once the heavy lifting

becomes a liability, it’s time for a harvest. After inundating

administrative assistants, collaborators, and family members,

we decided to sell the lab’s surplus honey to the public.

We built a small stand, located on the road that passes by

the lab, with pint jars of honey at $10 [AU$13.32/473ml]

each. So that we could tend to our research undisturbed, we

asked patrons to kindly deposit their payment into a lockbox

attached to the stand. In 2015, however, we noticed that

approximately 11% of the jars were being stolen. Really?

We’re getting robbed? Right here in Ithaca, NY? Therefore,

in 2016, we decided to do some casual research to answer

the question: Who steals from a honey stand?

Methods:

To collect our data, each member of the lab noted the

time, date, and number of jars put out for sale. As jars

disappeared, we collected and tallied the funds left in the

lockbox. Some jars were fully paid for ($10), some were

partially paid for ($1 to $9), and some were outright stolen

($0). With this data, we began to answer questions about

theft at the honey stand.

Question:

Are we getting robbed more often on the weekend?

Rationale:

Maybe people prefer to steal from us on the weekend,

when they think we’re not at the lab (we are). Or maybe

people steal during the week, because it provides some

excitement during the long workweek.
