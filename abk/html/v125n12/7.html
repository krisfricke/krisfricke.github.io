Australian Wins Apimondia

Honey Competition

This past September the world beekeeping congress, Apimondia, which

meets every two years, had their conference in Chile. While there were

various competitions which were decided at the time, the honey competition

results had to wait on testing.

160 honeys were presented from 29 countries, including three from

Australia. Nearly 70 judges conducted the traditional sensory evaluation

during the conference and based on their evaluation 61 nominees for awards

were determined. These 61 honeys were then sent for lab analysis to ensure

that the winning honeys met high standards of quality.

Honey had to be below 18% water content, 16 of the 61 honeys sent for

testing failed this criteria. Levels of HMF (See article by Clarence Collison in

this issue for a bit more on HMF), diastase and invertase were also tested.

They were also tested for chemical residues and 8 honeys failed these tests.

Two different mass spectrometry tests were used to test for sugar syrup

adulteration and 5 honeys failed on these grounds. Altogether 32 of the 61

honeys presented for analysis failed testing.

There were five categories in which awards were given: (1) monofloral

liquid (2) monofloral crystalized; (3) multifloral / forest liquid; (4) multifloral /

forest crystalized; and (5) comb honey.

In the category of liquid multifloral there was one gold medal, three silvers

and one bronze awarded, and we are proud to announce that Australia’s

Barbara Ki’a’i Weber won the gold and one of the silvers. Considering there

were only three Australian honeys submitted, this means two thirds of

Australian submissions won gold or silver.

According to Barbara the honey came from 15 hives on the edge of Mt

Jerusalem National Park, outer edge of Byron Shire, northern NSW. In the

vicinity on her property there is a variety of fruit trees, herbs and wildflowers,

and the national park has blackbutt, bloodwood, turpentine, cedar, blue

gum and acacia. She never has to feed her bees and there is no agriculture

or other apiaries near her. She had previously won silver at Apimondia 2019

and also at this most recent Apimondia her wax candles won gold in the

“decorated candles” category.

Barbara encourages us all: “In order to win a competition, first one must

dare to enter that competition. Even a world honey competition.”

For more information, just google “Apimondia honey competition,” the

top result will bring you to a one page summary with a link to the official full

report, which is clear and concise.

Small Hive Beetle

Eliminated From

Tasmania

In March 2023 Small Hive Beetles

(SHB)

were

detected

near

East

Devonport, Tasmania. The Department

of Natural Resources and Environment

Tasmania (NRE) declared a movement

restriction and set about endeavouring

the eliminate the invasive pest. After 12

months and thousands of inspections,

NRE is now declaring SHB to have

been eliminated from Tasmania
