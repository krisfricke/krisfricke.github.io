With the hives all snugly packed down for the winter

we can all relax and perhaps concentrate on other things,

surely? Maybe I get the next issue of this magazine all

together in a leisurely manner, happily warm in my house

while the frost persists on the grass outside till almost

noon some of these days

It was not to be, because I had the bright idea to attend

three state conferences in a row (Tasmania, Victoria,

WA) to find out exactly what’s going on in Tassie and

Westralia, and make some contacts. This was a great

success in that respect but that left me reading stacks of

papers and articles as the Spirit of Tasmania rocked her

way through the night across the Bass Straight,; excusing

myself from after-session drinks to hurry back to my

room to type something up; drawing diagrams for use in

articles on napkins during the speakers I’d already heard

at a previous conference. But by hook or by crook we got

there eventually and I’m actually very proud with what

has come together.

I was already working on an article about old comb

(rotate it out!), and then I came across an excellent article

on new comb (what encourages bees to make it, how you

can help them along) by Ross Conrad. Clarence Collison

has written about feral colonies, which somewhat ties in

to the letter-and-answer we have this time about feral

colony densities in Australia. Both Rod Bourke and Mary

Whitehouse have written articles on getting to know your

enemy / varroa. We have an article from Randy Oliver on

In Varroa news, the mite is now thoroughly pervasive

throughout the Sydney basin, and one detection in Bega

in south-east NSW is worrisome for Victoria. NSW DPI

has a new heatmap which you’ll find on page 8, with

(updated weekly so don’t sit there reloading it).

Now that I’ve finally got all the conferences behind me

and finally have this magazine complete … it’s time to

start thinking about next issue!

But you, hopefully you can now sit back, happily warm

in the house, with the frost glistening on the grass out
