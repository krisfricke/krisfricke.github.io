We performed the ceremonial reversal of the two hive

bodies a few weeks ago, and I always leave a weird amount

of honey supers at various degrees of fullness on my hives

all winter. Plus, I put on some winter patties and/or HiveAlive

fondant just to be sure….

I started last spring with these same three hives. My hives

are out on our back roof, and we were starting to get a

little worried about the amount of weight. My niece, Lindsey

Wood, who is an architect, was a little worried, too! So I’m

glad to be back to three hives. That was all I ever wanted,

and with working full time [at BetterBee, an American

beekeeping supply store] and now three granddaughters

between 1 and 6, and my other hobbies and such,

beekeeping was getting a bit too demanding. Sadly, I can’t

even remember how my hives went from 6 to 3, and that’s

not like me at all! Most likely just swarming and swarming,

because I always treat regularly to keep my bees as free of

varroa mites as is humanly possible.

My 8 frame wooden hive and one of my two BeeMax 10

frame hives But, I suppose that I should really start off with

the story I shared with my coworkers at a Betterbee Monday

morning meeting that they were still laughing about at that

Friday’s morning meeting. I wasn’t going to share it at all,

but I just HAD to.

Well, so Dennis and I had already taken off a few honey

supers from our three hives in mid September [equivalent

to Australian March], and I felt I was overdue for my third

varroa mite treatment of the season. I would ordinarily have

at least done a 72 hour mite drop count onto lightly oiled flat

trays, but I had not. This had been my beekeeping season of

neglect, after all.

My mite loads tend to start off slowly in the spring, perhaps

because of the location of the hives, but I knew they would

have built up by then. It is very rare to see varroa mites on

bees because they are so small, and because of how they

attach themselves up under the body parts of the bees and

are hidden.

I clumsily climbed out the window to the apiary on our

back roof and removed boxes down to the first hive body,

applied two Formic Pro pads, and closed it back up. Then I

did the same to my second 10 frame BeeMax [a brand of

polystyrene] hive. Finally, I approached the last of my three

hives on the left side of my roof. It was my “fancy hive,” an

eight frame wooden hive I’d splurged and bought a gabled

cedar roof for on a recent birthday, at the encouragement

of my coworkers!

I’ve been putting off beginning this article until I could be absolutely, totally sure

that my three honey bee hives had survived the winter and are flourishing. They

did and they are! It’s early spring now, and there is an appropriate amount of

activity, and each hive has plenty of room for expansion.

A happy little honey bee on the milk weed in our front

yard. Both my small front and back yards have become

deliberate pollinator havens over the years.
