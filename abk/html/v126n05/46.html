Editor’s note: the cited study is interesting and worth a read, it appears to have been well conducted by a truly unbiased

group of beekeepers. The test hives each had a sticky mat divided in two, with the “Bee Gym slim” installed over one side

and the other side as the control, reasoning the mites would drop straight down. Initially 50% more mites fell over the bee

gym side, though over the 18 week testing period for unknown reasons the margin of difference gradually decreased. But

altogether about 75% of the time the Bee Gym side had a higher mite drop than the control side. It should be noted that

this mite drop while benefi cial is probably not alone eliminating enough mites to reduce the population and should be used

in conjunction with –not instead of– the full regular panoply of cultural, mechanical and chemical mite control measures. The

study coordinator David Channon 2 sums it up best:

“I think if you look at the conclusions, this product is a tool, not a solution or a magic bullet. We’ve had varroa for

thirty years plus and are working towards breeding tolerance by selection (which comes with its own challenges) and

reducing chemical control usage which is where the gym fits in. I know the mite is new to Australian Beekeepers-

those that want to continue should know that all the quick fixes have been tried and failed. Current thinking here is

that the judicious use of brood breaks, chemicals and now bee gyms is the way forward whilst others go for breeding

for tolerance through selection (both natural and artificial). This will eventually produce a more varroa-tolerant bee

but with the collateral losses of colonies and weakening through brood damage and DWV en route.

My own experience following the selection route had to end as, while a degree of tolerance started to emerge,

over-selection for it resulted in a number of killer bee colonies of extraordinary ferocity and persistence. They boiled

out of the hive at the first whiff of smoke and remained in a highly alert and aggressive state for a good half hour over

a 200 meter radius. A lesson that selecting for a single trait in too focused a manner doesn’t always lead to success.

Others are proceeding more cautiously..”
