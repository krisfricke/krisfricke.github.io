Three planting guides for Western Australia

New Maintenance Assistant position

available at WBF Research Apiary

Community garden grows with grant support

Planting for pollinators in Western Australia is easier than ever thanks to new

Powerful Pollinator Planting Guides for the Jarrah Forest and Avon Wheatbelt regions

in south west Western Australia.

These new guides join the revamped Swan River guide, which now features an

extended list of native plants for gardeners in the Swan River region.

Powerful Pollinator Planting Guides are created by researchers to help gardeners,

farmers and land managers choose plants that attract pollinators to their properties.

Download the latest Powerful Pollinator Planting Guides

https://www.wheenbeefoundation.org.au/our-work/projects/powerful-pollinators/

Volunteers at Byron Youth Activity

Centre have been hard at work planting

native trees and shrubs to provide food

for pollinators in the local area.

The planting took place over several

days with help from Byron Community

College permaculture students, teachers,

youth workers, Byron Youth Centre staff,

and various community members.

The project focused on planting

native plants in and around Byron Youth

Activity Centre’s Food Forest. Inspired

by young people’s concerns for the

environment, the Food Forest was established in 2021 as a place for young people in

the community to connect with nature.

The native planting project was supported by a Carman’s Trees for Bees Community

Tree Grant. This grant program, in conjunction with the Wheen Bee Foundation,

supports community groups across the country to plant native trees and shrubs in

their local area to provide much-needed food for bees and other pollinators.

Read more about the Community Tree Grants program

https://www.wheenbeefoundation.org.au/our-work/projects/community-tree-grants/

The Wheen Bee Foundation’s Research Apiary at Richmond, New South

Wales requires a part-time Maintenance Assistant.

This is a paid role requiring about four hours each month to conduct regular

maintenance to ensure the environment is maintained in a showcase condition

so it is safe for the operators, safe for the bees and ready to host media, visitors

or potential donors.
