fit into the cavity – that’s why a deep hollow is so important.

Holes larger than 10 millimetres aren’t recommended because

there’s not many native bees that are that large, especially in

Melbourne.

While I’m away from home, my family take care of our bee

hotel. My dad regularly sends me updates, and I love heading

home and finding out who has moved in. My dad is also learning

a lot about bees now. Once everyone starts to learn about bees,

they always seem to want to know more.

My favourite bee is the Xylocopa, otherwise known as the

Carpenter bee10. They live for years and years, while some other

bee species only live for a matter of weeks. Carpenter bees also

have one of the furthest flying ranges of any bees, and they are

much better fliers than other species. They essentially fly until

they age and their wings start to degrade.

I grew up obsessed with nature documentaries – National

Geographic, Steve Irwin, David Attenborough, Malcolm Douglas

– I watched it all. I’ve always loved the outdoors and science

subjects. I’m lucky to now be combining my passions and doing

what I dreamed of when I was really young.

I was recently awarded a position as a Wattle Fellow11. It’s a

great program for those who are environmentally minded and

focused on sustainability, especially for those who want to be a

leader in making positive changes.

In future, I hope I can continue doing fieldwork, learning

from our Indigenous communities about caring for country

and getting to explore and protect the beautiful places around

Australia. I just love learning about everything.
