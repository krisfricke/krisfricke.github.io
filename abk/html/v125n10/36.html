Hope for our honey bees?

More perplexing has been the discovery of small ‘island

populations’ of Varroa-resistant Apis mellifera. These honey

bees have successfully selected themselves or have been

selected to live with Varroa (Table 2). As a rule of thumb,

these bees have not retained their Varroa tolerance when

translocated. Efforts to fix such a desirable trait has been

confounded by outbreeding that occurs when bees swarm

or where a colony supersedes its queen.

ARTICLE BY ALAN WADE, CANBERRA REGION BEEKEEPERS

Canada Bill [1847-1887], born in Yorkshire as William Jones, migrated to North America

first working the Mississippi River as a card sharp, later in the gambling dens on the

Union Pacific Railroad. Had anyone asked Canada Bill how to banish Varroa mites he

would have introduced himself gawkishly, proffered a very plausible and foolproof

solution and sought an upfront payment, a bargain for such considered advice.

As we are now discovering, mites and their virus partners

in crime are tricky creatures and are not so readily conned.

With many assurances that mites could be fully controlled

with synthetic miticides, beekeepers have everywhere

discovered that the mites actually knew better. They, the

mites, soon adapted and very quickly got into bed with the

viruses with a pact to make the situation worse for bees and

beekeepers alike.

Natural defences of other honey bees

But what solutions might honey bees provide? What can they

tell us about the defences they have put up in the course of

evolution and are continuing to learn about today.

Destructive mites and their apine hosts come in many colours.

First and foremost, we learn that bees and their parasites have

learnt to live with each other. The mites, like Canada Bill, learnt

to extract their wages of sin but not to kill their victims (Table 1).

Of these mites a few, notably Varroa destructor,

Tropilaelaps mercedesae, Tropilaelaps clareae and Ascarapis

woodi, have jumped species to the European honey bee

with devastating effect.
