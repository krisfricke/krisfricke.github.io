If you are not cleaning up, sorting and storing your equipment

The Codes objective is to reduce all that.

With the advent of varroa into Australia the amount of work

required to keep a hive alive has also increased dramatically, so

as part of the Transition to Management Program (T2M) the Code

was given an upgrade (along with the Biosecurity Manual) to align

with the new challenges beekeepers are facing (or soon may be)

in much of Australia.

Both these items, as well as an updated editable Appendix 1

form, can be found at honeybee.org.au/biosecurity.

Main changes from the original Code

So, what are the main changes from the original Code, and why

do they matter? I asked Bianca Giggins, Industry Development

Manager at AHBIC to give me a quick rundown of these changes

which she lists as follows.

“The simplest way I like to summarise is to view the new fillable

PDF Appendix 1.

The addition of 3 inspections for 3.1 & 3.2 are the biggest

changes, along with reducing the required number of hives needed

to follow the Appendix 1 down from 50 to 20 hives managed.”

PART B (Requirements for All Beekeepers)

Section 3

3.1 – inspect each hive in the apiary three times every year (up

from two previously), at a minimum of three calendar months

apart (was previously four months apart).

3.2 – In areas where varroa is not established, arthropod pest

(including varroa and Tropilaelaps mites) inspection of one hive

per apiary, three times per year, spring, summer & autumn, by

alcohol wash, soapy water wash, sugar-shake or drone uncap-

ping, recording the method/s used.

3.3 – In areas where varroa mite is present, more frequent

inspections and tests should be completed to determine

whether mite loads have reached treatment thresholds. –THIS

POINT IS NEWLY ADDED.

Section 5

5.1 b) What Varroa mite test method was used including date of

test and test results as per Part B 3.2.

5.1 c) Details of all treatments and actions taken to manage any

pests or diseases in the apiary.

PART C (Beekeepers with 20 or more hives now have to follow all

conditions set out in Part C)

9.2 a) Biosecurity for Beekeepers Training (BOLT) must be com-

pleted within 12 months of first registering and every three

years afterwards”

So why does any of this matter to a recreational

beekeeper?

As many of you may have heard or seen for yourselves the

introduction of varroa has very much changed beekeeping for all

of us, with many losing their hives and many others losing hope to

carry on with keeping bees.

Varroa makes it very difficult for some beekeepers who per-

haps don’t have enough time, skills or luck to manage mite levels

well enough and keep their bees alive, but at the same time many

others have concentrated on managing varroa and mostly forgot-

ten about all the other pests and diseases that were already here

before varroa.

Many of these older issues are still a major threat to bee health

and the Code has increased the requirement of brood inspections

so that beekeepers are more compelled to manage their brood box

adequately, because we all know that under the old Code that two

brood inspections per year was not nearly enough to always run

good bees.

Bee Biosecurity training is always an important aspect for man-

aging better healthier bee colonies, and it is concerning that even

after varroa has been present for 4+ years that there are so many

beekeepers who still haven’t undertaken any training at all. The

Bee BOLT training is free to all beekeepers as the BBO programs

fund its yearly operating costs, so please undertake it, even if you

only have one hive or are new to beekeeping.

By reducing the Appendix 1 requirement hive numbers from 50

to 20 hives, the Code has been able to encompass literally thou-

sands of additional beekeepers who have been running more hives

than they probably should have without getting additional training

skills, as well as compelling them to be looking for AFB more thor-

oughly.

Bee Biosecurity Officers are here to assist beekeepers to under-

stand and follow the Code, so if you need assistance with any area

of the Code please contact your local BBO.
