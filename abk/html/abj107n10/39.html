Bendigo Branch Report

By Jamie Trimby

W

e’ve had a warm winter through Central Victoria. As a

result cape weed (Arctotheca calendula) flowered two to

three weeks early, at the start of September, canola crops

to the east of Bendigo were three to four weeks early, whilst bot-

tlebrush (Melaleuca viminalis), which on an average year sees buds

breaking by the last week in September, appears to be later than

usual. Consistent spells of rain through August to mid-September

have proliferated a good variety of pollen sources (visibly diverse

colours in hives) resulting in swarms from the 28th of August.

Increasing warm winds in the third week of September significantly

reduced soil moisture in Bendigo and surrounding areas, finally

evaporating the flood water to the north of Bendigo, but which

might slow bee population growth a little. Please ensure your bees

have access to safe fresh water as the increasing brood will have

greater water requirements.

Stafford, Angelique, and I presented on spring hive and swarm

management at the September meeting to a full house. A number

of beekeepers travelled hours to participate, and the blend and

breadth of experience in the room allowed insightful questions,

answers, and general wide discussion of variations within apiaries

through the changes in the seasons. Everyone involved continues

learning together.

We put on a hands-on spring management training at the

BBVAA apiary, which, thanks to Doug and Stafford, still has all four

of its hives. Several participants practised mite wash tests (average

10 per hive). Angelique and I went through the hives, demonstrat-

ing how to check the brood for diseases, signs of queenrightness or

swarming, and how to provide additional space for growing popu-

lations. Angelique also gave a dry-run demonstration of oxalic acid

vaporisation, which sparked plenty of discussion and tips on using

an InstantVap safely, hazards, cleaning, and where to source com-

pliant respirators and filters. Due to the need for respirators we

had to wait until everyone departed and then did a live vape to get

the varroa numbers down, with a plan to later add Aluen Cap strips

as well.

The 45th Annual Beekeepers Field Day will have been held at

Harcourt on 11 October by the time you are reading this. BBVAA

committee members put a great deal of work into planning and

organising the Field Day every year. This year, alongside regular

contributors Stafford and Peta, Kris Barker and Jess Ruedin shoul-

dered a lot of the responsibility. A magnificent effort, well done.

The BBVAA will have a stall at the Bendigo Agricultural Show on

the 23rd and 24th of October at the Prince of Wales Showgrounds,

Bendigo. Club members will be promoting the importance of the

Victorian beekeeping industry to the general public, our observa-

tion hive, bee talk, the benefits of being in a club, plus the sweet

taste of all their efforts

We are already looking forward to our Christmas Party on

Sunday the 22nd of November with our annual honey competition.

This is a wonderful social day, an opportunity to acquire the moral

entitlement to boast about how awesome your honey is, catch up

with like-minded friends and, paired with delicious food, makes for

a great way to reflect on the year.
