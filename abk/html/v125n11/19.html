Bee clubs, do you have an International Bee Day event

of some kind? It’s a great opportunity to encourage

people to join and support your club.

You can search for or add events to the list of World

Bee Day events at worldbeeday.org.au , and the website

as well has lots of suggestions for how to celebrate the

day and resources such as a free downloadable media

pack and pdf posters.

Some talking points: at least 53 Australian crops

depend on honeybee pollination, including most fruit,

and even livestrock via the pollination of the lucerne they

feed on. If Australian beekeeping suffers a reduction in

scale, it will drive up the costs of all these products.

In a study cited by the UN Food and Agriculture

Organization, pollination of small farms increased crop

yields by an average of 24 percent1. The United Nations

recommends celebrating World Bee Day by encouraging

the following:

Individuals:

•

planting a diverse set of native plants, which flower

at different times of the year;

•

buying raw honey from local farmers;

•

buying products from sustainable agricultural

practices;

•

avoiding pesticides, fungicides or herbicides in our

gardens;

•

protecting native bee colonies when possible;

•

sponsoring a hive;

•

making a bee water fountain by leaving a water

bowl outside;

•

helping sustaining forest ecosystems;

•

raising awareness around us by sharing this

information within our communities and networks;

The decline of bees affects us all!

Farmers:

•

reducing, or changing the usage of pesticides;

•

diversifying crops as much as possible, and/or

planting attractive crops around the field;

•

creating hedgerows.

Governments and decision-makers:

•

strengthening

the

participation

of

local

communities in decision-making, in particular

that of indigenous people, who know and respect

ecosystems and biodiversity;

•

enforcing strategic measures, including monetary

incentives to help change;

•

increasing collaboration between national and

international organizations, organizations and

academic and research networks to monitor and

evaluate pollination services.

To the above lists I would add:

Beekeepers:

•

Give a talk at a local community group or school

•

Use the opportunity to advertise / encourage the

buying of your local honey on the day

•

Participate in the Wheen Bee Foundation’s Great Bee

Morning Tea (“Gather your friends, colleagues or

classmates and hold a morning tea filled with treats

made entirely with 100 percent pure Australian

honey.”)

•

Encourage your local Farmer’s Market to participate in

the “Bring Your Buzz” campaign which “encourages

people to wear yellow to their local farmers’ markets

and buy food from producers who value the role bees

play in their production.” from May 18th – 26th.

Clubs:

•

Make sure your local paper knows about the day and

encourage them to support it, perhaps mention that

an article about the local beekeeping community

would be very timely.

•

Encourage your local Farmer’s Market to support the

abovementioned “Bring Your Buzz” campaign.

•

Hold a public event in the local community.

See more at https://www.worldbeeday.org.au/ ,

hosted by the Wheen Bee Foundation.
