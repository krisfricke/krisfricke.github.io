Bee Swarm Collection and Removal

Please note - The Geelong Beekeepers Club is NOT a bee removal service. We MAY be able to assist depend-

ing on the circumstances, but as a group of volunteers, it's not possible for us to help in all instances.

Is it a swarm or an established/new hive?

Understanding the difference between a bee swarm and a hive or nest can determine the appropriate service

you require for the bees to be removed safely.

Bee Swarms

Bee swarms are identified by a mass of bees clustered on a post or hanging from a tree or some other

structure. The bees may stay in this cluster up to a few days while they look for a new home.

We recommend you contact one of the organisations or members listed on the club website as soon as

possible, especially in relation to swarm bees, before they settle into a permanent place in your home - "Give

them a call before they get into your wall".

Established Hives/Colonies

Bees moving in and out of a building wall, roof or chimney could indicate an established colony depending on

how long they have been there.

If you can see honeycomb or the bees have been coming and going more than a week, chances are a hive is

either well established or starting to establish.

The Geelong Beekeepers Club is a volunteer organisation and has limited resources to assist with the

removal of swarms and in particular established colonies. To assist the public with the safe removal or

relocation of bee swarms and colonies, the above resources have been provided.

For Hive removal . .

To remove an established colony from inside a building (called a "Cut out") requires specialist skills and

equipment as well as appropriate insurance. A suitably qualified Bee Removal Specialist will most likely

charge a fee to remove the hive in this case.

Bee colonies can also exist out in the open where you will also see the honeycomb.

What can you do if you suspect an established hive in a building structure?

Request help from a local beekeeper from the options listed previously.

Engage a Bee Removal Specialist such as Bee Removals Victoria - https://beeremovals.com.au/

Club members who collect swarms

A list of Geelong Beekeepers Club members who may be able to collect free-hanging and accessible swarms

in the Geelong district, with their areas and costs, is kept on the club website's Swarm Removal page. Most

do not do "cut outs".

For a beekeeper to determine their ability and capacity to assist you, it is helpful to provide the following in-

formation:

Location (including suburb and where the hive is living such as inside a wall, chimney, roof or type of

container such as compost bin)

Provide a photo if safe to do so

State how long the bees have been there

Contact details such as phone or email

Depending on the difficulty or distance travelled to remove the bees, it is not unreasonable for the

beekeeper to charge for their services. If it is an easy swarm removal, most beekeepers will provide a free

service. We recommend asking the beekeeper if a fee will be charged before accepting their assistance.
