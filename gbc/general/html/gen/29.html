GENERAL BEEKEEPING

7 ways to find the queen

(And one way to get a result without finding the queen)

1. Go the centre

Use minimal smoke, 1–2 puffs; take off the honey super and queen excluder.

Space frames apart in the middle of the brood box.

Scrape off burr comb on the side of the top bar.

Carefully lift one frame out of the middle, taking care not to squash bees.

Briefly scan over each side of this frame (like reading, taking about 1 minute). Place this frame in a spare

box and continue with the next frame.

The queen can be on the wall of the hive body or on the bottom board. The centre of the brood nest, on a

frame with eggs, is the most likely place to find her. Speed is an advantage because the old queen can hide

well. Have an empty cage handy: if you find her on the first frame you might recycle her into a drone layer or

an eggless hive later, if she has a good brood pattern or other good qualities. Once you've found her, shake

bees off combs, check for disease, manipulate combs, scratch honey in corners to make egg-laying space, lift

honey out to the top and replace with empty brood combs, introduce the cage and mark the front of the box

with code – date – breeder (W123).

2. Go the outside

Otherwise known as the light barrier or Linton Briggs method.

Place an empty box next to the hive.

Use minimal smoke, 1–2 puffs; take off the honey super and queen excluder.

With the sun over your left shoulder, remove the outside frame furthest from you, checking for the

queen before placing it in the empty box.

Remove the outside frame closest to you, again checking for the queen before placing it in the spare box.

While it is not usual to find the queen on the outside frames (it can happen), removing these frames first

creates a light barrier between the next frame and the hive wall. This confines the queen to the remaining

frames.

Before checking both sides of the next frame closest to you, glance down the face of the frame.

According to Linton the queen often stands taller than the rest of the bees, and can be spotted up to

20% of the time on the face of the frame before it is removed. Repeat for the remaining frames until the

queen is found.

3. Divide and conquer

Put an empty box next to the hive.

Take out half the frames and place them in the empty box. Put empty combs in each box.

You will then have 4 frames of bees and brood, and 4 frames of pollen, honey and empty combs, in each

box.
