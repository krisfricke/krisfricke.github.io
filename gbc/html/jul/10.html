Almond pollination prices seem to vary greatly from $170 to $250 per hive. It is predicted that

80,000 hives are short for this season.

Tocal college are going to conduct Chemical User courses as beekeepers using chemical varroa

treatments need to have completed this course.

Honey prices have increased at the honey packers.

Honey flows for next season look scarce in Queensland and New South Wales for the forthcoming

season I am told. The floral sources seem to be coming into flower slightly earlier than usual.

Spanish Heath a noxious weed of wet areas is flowering and the bees are strong and making brood.

Sallow Wattle has its first flowers at Aireys Inlet but only budded around Geelong. Mount Morgan

wattle has the first flowers opening.

Yellow Gum is flowering but will most likely finish flowering before the warm spring weather. Red

Box has an excellent budding. Messmate appears to be very patchy with some areas looking good

but many other areas not budded.

Canola crops vary from just small seedlings out of the ground to nice small plants locally.

Farmers have been spraying Canola crops for Earwigs. I had to shift bees due to spraying.

Caterpillars abound in the Mallee and it could possibly be a Bogong moth season. They walk over

the flowers at night and leave a scent that repels the bees.

Mice could become a problem as their numbers increase. Reduced entrances help keeping them

out of the hive.

The following snippets have been taken from the March 2026 Beekeepers Quarterly

Yellow-legged Asian hornets are spreading in England. In USA Georgia and South Carolina have

been difficult to eradicate due to marshy and Savanah areas.

USA colony losses of over 60% of commercial bee hives in 2025. High levels of acute paralysis

virus and deformed wing virus were found along with resistance to Amitraz (Apivar) which are

thought to be the problems.

European beekeepers staged a conference to understand the biology of varroa resistance.

Examples of Treatment free beekeeping were given by Kirk Webster from Vermont USA and the

wife Anita Reinsertsen whose late husband kept 400 beehives for over 25 years treatment free.

Discussions regarding breeding and selection for varroa resistance and open mated locally adapted

queens. More conferences will be held in 2026 in Poland, Germany, and Austria.

Bees prefer to eat freshly stored pollen. Bee bread is produced by a two-stage process of firstly

Lactobacillus Kunkeei which metabolizes the fructose sugars in the mixed pollen and nectar. The

second stage involves fermentation using the yeast Saccharomyces. The yeasts help preserve the

bee bread. Nurse bees convert the bee bread into brood food. Exposure to crop spraying

fungicides will destroy yeast cells and allowing the bee bread to spoil. Formic acid used to control

varroa can disrupt the PH conditions for some yeast and bacterial species.

A commercial beekeeper made an interesting comment that when bees cap over pollen it most

likely is contaminated by fungicides.???? I thought they put a cap of honey over it turn it into bee

bread and then capped it for future use.

I see wattles forming buds, and at Torquay the Cape Wattle has been flowering for a few weeks.

This wattle is very weedy but is odd in that it yields nectar and pollen.

Protein patties need to be placed on top of the brood frames in late autumn before the weather

gets cold. If a cold snap occurs like this season the bees ignore it as they reduce brood nests.

Timing is very important.
