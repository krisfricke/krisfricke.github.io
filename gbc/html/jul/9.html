Seasonal Report

July 2026

By John Edmonds

The weather has been erratic winter weather. Fortunately, some patchy

rain has fallen to germinate the Canola crops. Locally the Geelong area

has been dry, but areas in the South West and North West have enjoyed

almost too much rain and have excellent breeding conditions at present.

Around Mildura since March 13 inches of rain has fallen and resulted in

the best breeding conditions ever seen. With Wards Weed, Wild Mustard

and Turnip knee high. Today 17/6/2026 was 22 degrees which is 6

degrees above the average. The nights have been chilly.

Twin Leaf has started flowering and creates excellent breeding conditions. Barrel Clover will be

good this spring and is eagerly sought after by the bees. I expect swarming could be a problem

especially during almond pollination.

My friend took his hives to the Desert Banksia but unfortunately, they are not doing very much. I

guess the dry autumn may be the problem. South Australian beekeepers report the same problem.

A Queensland beekeeper that has his bee hives on the Napunyah in outback Queensland channel

country has a good honey flow and full bees but cannot get to them due to flooding. With more rain

coming and almond pollination looming it may be having to shift the bees by helicopter at $1400 per

hour cost.

Due to the weather being warmer than usual in Queensland. Eucalyptus trees that normally flower in

summer have commenced flowering. The Yellow Box tree opposite my property has commenced

flowering, hopefully others do not follow suit.

Some areas in the Mallee region have Canola crops coming into flower. Locally it is still only small

plants that should flower beginning of August. I see the canola is very patchy in its germination this

year, possibly the sowing into dry soil has been a problem. Eastern Grey Kangaroos are grazing the

young plants and seem to be increasing their numbers rapidly.

I was unable to attend the VAA conference but my friend reported that the speech by Steve Martin a

British varroa researcher gave us optimism for the future. Fortunately, so far, the Australian varroa

does not have “Deformed Wing Virus” this is the bee killer. Without this virus hives should be able to

contend with up to 3,000 varroa mites in a hive. If we select the best preforming bee hives to breed

from, it is possible that varroa resistant bees will be possible. This could be achieved in five years. I

find this interesting as a commercial beekeeper in Victoria has not treated his bees or even looked

for varroa despite being in an area infected by varroa. He has more than 80 tonnes of honey

harvested from his hives this season. It will be interesting to see how the hives survive the winter.

Queensland beekeepers have had their bee hives in the Channel country and gained a crop of

Napunyah honey. They purchased Faba Bean Flour from me as the available pollen is very poor.

The bees worked open drums on their sides of Faba Bean flour and Ultra Bee supplement side by

side and the bees gathered both feeds slightly favoring the Faba Bean Flour. I fed my hives patties

made with Faba Bean flour, sugar, Soy flour and Agrisea. Only scraps of the patties are left in the

hives. It is important to place the patties directly over the brood frames in the center of the brood

nest before the bees shut down for the season. When the weather becomes cold the bees lose

interest in consuming the patties.
