In the Zone(s)

Central South

Bees in our hives are busy collecting nectar and pollen whenever there is a sunny spot, even with

temperatures in the low teens. Bottom trays have been inspected regularly and no sign of Varroa

found yet. There have been no significant indications of pests, only some ants that are getting into

some people's hives, even though they keep refilling the ant traps.

Natalie Lee

North West Zone

General Hive Activity: Members reported excellent hive activity during the warmer days in late July

and early August. When conditions are warm enough, all reported seeing strong foraging behaviour

from their colonies.

Forage Sources: Our beekeepers have been observing diverse pollen sources:

Wattle (yellow pollen) and ironbark (creamy white pollen) were noted, sparking a detailed

discussion about wattle species and their beekeeping value

Ash trees flowering near the apiary have been actively worked by local hives

One member is planting additional bee-friendly trees to strengthen their forage resources

Varroa Monitoring: Members have been vigilant following the club's varroa spotting session. One

beekeeper's varroa numbers remain stable, whilst another deployed sticky mats on their Langstroth

hives. Several beekeepers may need glasses upgrades after attempting to spot varroa at the last

meeting

Hive Maintenance & Projects: One member has been productive, making nuc boxes, fixing the roof

and repainting one of their Long Lang hives.

A fellow beekeeper successfully sharpened hive tools using a sharpening wheel (with sensible

safety precautions!)

Another member constructed an ingenious honey melter using an insulated box, 100W reptile

heating globe and thermostat set to 40°C. This is capable of liquefying a full 10-litre bucket of

crystallised honey overnight

Ian Ashman
