Seasonal Report

August 2026

John Edmonds

The start of July is cold winter weather with much needed rain. The area around

Geelong has now received enough moisture to promote growth in Canola and

pasture plants and weeds. Even on the cold days we enjoy a few hours of sunshine

in the afternoon. It is amazing to see how quickly the Canola plantlets have developed from little 2

to 4 leaves to now small plants. It is pleasing to see the attitude of farmers now appreciating the

benefit of honey bee pollination. A farmer calculated that the bees would increase his yield by 100

tonnes. A farmer said “you can put your bees anywhere but not in the backyard as Mum would not

like that.”

8/7/2026 Today I was amazed at the quick growth of the Canola plants, good rainfall, warm soil and

sunny afternoons spurring the growth. I expect an earlier than usual flowering. Swans are still

grazing on the young Canola plants but I see the farmer parks his Ute’s in the middle of where the

swans feast. They will not go near the vehicles. Eastern Grey Kangaroos love eating Canola and

each year I see greater numbers as they breed prolifically. I have kept bees on this farm since 1982

and it has only been over the past 10 years that 3 or 4 kangaroos have now become multiple mobs

of up to 70 kangaroos of all sizes.

I enjoy looking for the Brolga birds. About four years ago only a pair were present and had a pair of

offspring. This spring I counted nine Brolgas in the group. They are very tame and not scared of

people taking photos and watching them. Being light grey they are hard to see as they wander

through the paddocks finding grain, insects, mice, frogs and even eat roots of plants. Just on dusk I

have seen them dancing to impress their partner.

It seems the Sugar Gum this season was a failure, and bees just survived.

Reports of Canola crops that have started flowering in the northern areas of Victoria will give the

bees a boost prior to Almond pollination. I doubt that the hives will have had a brood break this

winter, a few quick checks have discovered at least 2 frames of brood. I expect hives to be very

variable depending on the autumn floral conditions and quality of the queen. A Mildura beekeeper

told me that he has hives with 6 frames of brood and doubles full of bees at the start of July. I

expect varroa will have been breeding to higher numbers. Good luck stopping them swarming on

the Almond pollination.

Soursob has started flowering and I find it hard to evaluate how much benefit the bees gain from it.

It can yield a thin nectar and pollen and breed the bees. I think the weather is very important to

whether the bees can harvest it. This weed has increased its spread greatly over the past few years.

My neighbor blamed rabbits for spreading it.

It seems the trees are mixed up and some flowering early, Bottlebrushes, Manchurian Pears , Yellow

Box and Red Box. Thankfully most are still holding their budding.

When I inspect the beehives, I mark any that are aggressive, so they can be requeened. I have

noticed that often the next time they are well behaved, why the difference? An article in the June

American Beekeeping Journal reports that bees that are hungry are likely to have nasty attitudes.

They refer to these bees as “hangry”. (A combination of Hungry and angry.)
