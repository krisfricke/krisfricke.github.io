Around where I live, we are seeing the rapid expansion of Geelong with new houses being built.

The developers knock down the existing trees and make a bare paddock to sub divide.

Unfortunately, I observe that the new houses take up nearly all the allotment and the gardens have

very little floral sources for bees to forage. A mixture of trees are planted in the nature strips and

some species will be valuable for honey bees. Many areas of Geelong have had new plantings of

Manchurian Pears which flower early spring and seem to only provide pollen. I suspect they may

increase swarming. When I travel through Werribee’s new suburbs they have excellent honey

yielding trees planted in the many reserves and nature strips. Yellow Box, Grey Box , Red Box, Red

Gum , Yellow Gum, Spotted Gum, and Ironbark. This should be done in all Victorian new housing

suburbs.

A very interesting article in the June 2026 American Bee Journal by Randy Oliver the American

scientist beekeeper. He had been testing the PH levels of Oxalic acid treatments on the surface of

bees after oxalic acid treatments. He found anomalies in the experiment results. He concluded that

it was caused by bee venom being acidic. It was neutralizing the oxalic acid. Further reviewing

research papers revealed that worker bees smear bee venom over themselves during self-

grooming. Bee venom is antimicrobial and contains peptides and melittin. It was concluded that

venom bathing may be used by bees to reduce varroa infestation. It was found that bees that did

not have varroa had less venom on themselves. Queens and Drones did not have a coating of

venom. The question is do Apis Cerana also use venom to protect themselves? There is variations in

varroa mite fertility, could this be due to bee venom? I have found Bee venom ointment to be useful

in reducing pain from arthritis.

As I travel around Geelong, I see Lemon Scented Gums budded for late spring flowering. Nearly

every year these street trees flower and provide an excellent light honey. The Spotted Gum is not

consistently budded this year, but some trees look good. The bees gather pollen but I have never

noticed the very dark honey in the hives. This tree is sought after in New South Wales as it is a very

good yielder and makes strong bees. My friend took his bees to the Spotted Gum at Batemans Bay

and harvested honey but paid dearly for it with many stings. He was amazed at how many Small

Hive Beetles invaded his hives.
