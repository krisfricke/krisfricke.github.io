The club has also prepared a suite of presentation materials to support members who wish to

engage with community groups and speak on behalf of the Geelong Beekeepers Club. These

resources ensure our volunteers feel confident, well‑equipped and consistent in the information

they share, helping us maintain a clear, evidence‑based message wherever we are

represented. World Bee Day activities again generated strong interest, reminding us that fascination

with bees extends well beyond our membership.

Media engagement increased this past 12 months, with both Stephen Fridey and myself appearing

on Pulse FM’s Local Legends program. These interviews provided excellent opportunities to

promote the club and highlight the importance of bees. We were also fortunate to secure five weeks

of community service announcements across 3GL, K‑Rock and Bay FM, aired three times daily.

Geelong Broadcasters generously extended this with an additional month of airtime on Bay FM in

July. This level of exposure helped introduce the club to thousands of people across the region.

A significant milestone this past year was awarding Life Membership to Helmut Woerner atrthe

August 2025 meeting. His contributions to education, mentoring, technical support and community

outreach have had a lasting impact on the club. Whether building observation hives, assisting at

workshops or helping new beekeepers find their feet, Helmut has consistently given his time and

expertise for the benefit of others. The recognition was thoroughly deserved.

Our club apiary also saw its share of challenges. Due to roofing works at The Heights, our hives

spent part of the year at Batesford under the care of Stephen and Susan Fridey before returning

home. Thanks to the efforts of several committee members, the hives now sit on improved

foundations with better access and working space.

Our infrastructure and resources continued to grow. We secured a City of Greater Geelong 2025–

26 Community Equipment Grant ($1,445.41), enabling the purchase of a new Flow Hive for training

and demonstration. We expanded our library, improved website content, increased digital resources

and continued investing in educational materials such as Hello Honey Bee flyer.

This past year also saw the introduction of paid advertising opportunities in our newsletter, with

support from Bec’s Beehives and Aluen CAP. I would also like to thank John Edmonds (Edmonds

Honey) and Stan Filippis (Stan’s Maleebee Farm) for their ongoing support to the club, their

generosity plays an important role in the club’s success.

None of these achievements happen without volunteers. Behind every course, speaker event, hive

opening, display stand, newsletter article, website update and committee meeting is a member

giving their time to help others. The strength of our club has never been measured by the number

of hives we collectively manage, it has always been the people who are willing to share knowledge,

lend a hand and contribute to something larger than themselves.

As we look ahead, the challenges facing beekeeping remain significant. Varroa will continue to test

us all. But if the past year has shown anything, it is that our club is well positioned to meet those

challenges through education, collaboration and community.

Thank you to every committee member, volunteer, presenter, mentor and member who contributed

throughout the year. Your efforts continue to make the Geelong Beekeepers Club one of the

strongest and most active beekeeping clubs in Victoria.

I am proud of what we have achieved together and excited for what lies ahead. And yes, rumours

suggest that live streaming of our club meetings may not be far away in fact I watched the July

meeting remotely to help test the technologies. 

Nathan Whitford

President, Geelong Beekeepers Club Inc.
