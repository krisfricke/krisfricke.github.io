Members Area Zones

Our Zones are NORTH WEST, NORTH EAST, CENTRAL

North, CENTRAL South, SOUTH WEST and EAST. We

encourage members to actively participate in their Zone

where members can get help and offer help to other

members.

Some Zones organise various activities such as

shared hive openings or workshops.

With the arrival of Varroa, we believe Zones will be

become more important in helping members share

resources, ideas, treatments and help.

Zone leaders can only contact members who agree to be contacted.

If you would like to be involved in Zone activities, update your profile "Share with leader"

section.

"Members near you" link will (subject to conditions**) show your location in the centre of a map as

well as other member locations near you.

If other members have agreed to share their contacts, those details will be shown when you

hover over an icon.

** To be able to view the approximate locations of other members hives, you will need to

agree to show the location of your own hives. Update your profile and click 'Yes' for the "Show

my hive location" option.

To be able to see the contact details of other members who have shared their details, you will

also need to share your details. Update your profile and enter your telephone and/or email in

the "Show these contact details" field.

After changing your profile and saving, you will need to logout and then log back in again for the change

to be active.

Whilst we are encouraging members to make their contact details available to other members in the spirit

of mentoring and sharing information, it is certainly not mandatory.

Also, members who do share their contact details are under no obligation to provide assistance if

contacted by other areas.

Zone News

NW Zone Varroa Update by Ian Ashman

As NW Zone members undertook their first full inspections of spring, varroa monitoring at Inverleigh

apiaries during late August revealed varied results, with mite levels remaining low overall. Soapy water

wash testing showed positive progress: three hives recorded zero mites (including one previously

identified as positive in April), three hives had one mite each, and one stronger hive had three mites. The

important takeaway is that varroa prevalence appears patchy across the zone. Interestingly, drone

uncapping emerged as an effective screening tool for early detection, sometimes revealing mites before

standard alcohol or soapy water washes can detect them. Members were reminded that treatment is not

yet required for these apiaries, with most still having at least one to two months before intervention

becomes necessary.

Spring inspections revealed strong hive conditions overall, with most apiaries showing solid brood

patterns, abundant pollen reserves, and excellent bee activity. Beekeepers reported significant pollen

collection from local wattle and canola sources, indicating good forage availability.

Several practical techniques were discussed, including regular hive weight monitoring as a reliable method

for assessing winter survival and spring build-up without opening the hive excessively. With swarming

season approaching, beekeepers were encouraged to remain vigilant about prevention strategies and to

share any questions or swarm management experiences with the wider zone.
