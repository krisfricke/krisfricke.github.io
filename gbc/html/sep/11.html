Varroa is showing up around Geelong. Reports and customers purchasing Oxalic Acid strips from

Inverleigh, Teesdale, Winchelsea, Moriac, Forrest, Anglesea, Hamilton, Little River, Werribee,

Werribee South, Corio, and Balliang.

One can conclude that nowhere is safe from Varroa now. Especially as swarming occurs.

Beekeepers catching and shifting swarms to new areas will transfer the varroa.

Customers are purchasing the Oxalic Acid varroa treatment strips. I think this is a safe method as it

does not contaminate the honey or beeswax. You must wear nitrile gloves as the acid will soak into

leather gloves and possibly burn your hands. Do not have it come into contact with your eyes. I

have been told of New South Wales beekeepers that have used the strips back-to-back for three

years successfully. I do not like the Vaping of Oxalic Acid due to the possibility of accidental damage

to the beekeepers lungs and eyes and forming kidney stones. Cheap imported vaporizers off the

internet may block up the nozzle quickly and run out of battery power after only a few hives. Also, it

seems that the procedure needs to be done every six days and often the Varroa numbers remain

high. A friend vaped his hives at Inverleigh and the dead mites on the sticky mat were too many to

count, described as "thousands".

I have been uncapping drone brood as it is quicker than washes and the varroa stand out on the

white pupae and can be easily seen, often walking around. So far, I have found two hives that had

large numbers of Varroa despite other bee hives in the apiary having only low counts. I guess that

these bee hives have robbed out dying bee hives and been reinfected. Bee hives located at Ocean

Grove, Grovedale and You Yangs have so far been free of Varroa.

It seems the drones are spreading the Varroa to other bee hives and especially those that have had

virgin queens in them and have attracted the drones. I have never seen in books where any

comments have been made about this.

An apiary located between Teesdale and Inverleigh had six bee hives with Varroa in the drone

brood and thirty bee hives that were still clean. It was interesting to note the infected bee hives

faced to the west or were on the corners of the apiary. Perhaps the drones flew in from the west.

I think removal of frames containing drone comb will be important (30% drop) and screen bottoms

(20% drop). I have seen an interesting idea to make a screen rim that fits on top of the existing solid

bottom board and allows a corflute board coated with vegetable oil to slide in to monitor the varroa

drop.

I am inspecting hives and recording the mite counts. Strangely I have found hives that had mites in

washes in May 2026 but now I cannot find mites in the drone brood. Unless the hive is heavily

Varroa infested it is unlikely you will find Varroa in worker brood.

Experts say that the first 2 to 3 years need constant vigilance, but after then the feral and

unmanaged hives will have died and honey flows are much greater. Three treatments per year are

predicted to be sufficient.

Varroa has been in overseas countries for 40 years The European Union, Argentina, and United

Kingdom and Turkey yet they make significant honey yields each year. We just have to learn how to

deal with it.
