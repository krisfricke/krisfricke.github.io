Another method for making queen cells is to have a double decker hive with only 4 frames of honey, pollen

and sealed brood, the rest of the box has 4 follower boards (timber cut into the shape of frames that reduce

the space in the box). The bottom board has a mesh base to allow the bees air. The entrance is closed. The

young bees from a strong double hive are shaken into the bottom box the older flying bees will return home

to the queen. The bees will cover the 4 frames and the rest hang as a cluster like a swarm under the frames

into the empty chamber below. The graft of queen cells is introduced and the bees kept locked in for 2 days

in a cool place (often inside a shed).

The bees panic and start making queen cells. The queen cells can then be transferred to a strong bee hive

that has a super over a queen excluder with honey and pollen frames and the center has unsealed brood to

attract the nurse bees through the queen excluder. The bees will finish the queen cells. It is thought that this

method makes the best queens.

Cloake method uses a strong double decker hive that has a queen excluder and cloake board which has a

slide and upstairs entrance. The slide can be inserted making the upper super queen less, the bees will start

drawing the queen cells and then after 2 days the slide is removed to allow the hive become a complete unit

and finish making the queen cells.
