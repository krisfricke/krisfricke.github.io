Requeening an aggressive hive

Deb Smith · Pages 22–23 · General Beekeeping

Depopulating the Brood Box Method

EQUIPMENT NEEDED

Spare base and lid

Spare box (full depth box if your brood box is full depth)

New queen (preferably mated in a cage from a reputable breeder)

Notes before you start

In this scenario, the hive is made up of a full depth brood box, queen excluder and honey super. Therefore,

you know before you start, the queen is in the bottom brood box. If you have more than one honey super on

the hive, ensure all honey supers are left in the original hive location and you are only moving the brood box

away from the area to find the queen. Requeening is more efficient with two sets of eyes. Engage the help of

a friend if you can!

METHOD

Smoke the hive as usual.

Depending on the space around your hive, either move the whole hive back a little and rotate 180

degrees, or if moving the brood box 20 metres away at this point, move the whole hive to the side.

Place the spare base board in the original hive location.

Place the honey super on the spare base board and add the spare lid.

Place the hive lid on the brood box.

Depending on your decision to either rotate the hive 180 degrees or move the brood box 20 metres or so

in the above step, follow one of the applicable steps below:

(a) If you have turned the brood box 180 degrees, leave the box for a couple of hours so the foraging

bees fly back to the original hive. This will depopulate the brood box and you will have far less bees to

deal with to find the queen. The foraging bees tend to have the attitude too. After a couple of hours,

move the brood box away to find the queen.

(b) If you are moving the brood box away from the original location, use a wheelbarrow or trolley to assist

with easy movement, then leave the brood box for a couple of hours.

After a couple of hours, it's time to find the old queen. There are several ways to do this. My preference is

to start searching on the outer frames and work my way towards the centre. Have your spare box set up

next to the brood box on top of the hive lid so you can place checked frames in this box. With the sun

over your shoulder, start with the closest frame and check the dark side first which is the opposite side to

what you see when you lift the frame. Scan your eye beginning in the top left corner and move across the

frame, then continue back across to the left until you're satisfied, she's not there. Turn the frame over and

check the other side. Place the checked frame in the spare box, same location. Continue checking frames

in the following number order until you find the queen: 8, 1, 7, 2, 6, 3, 5, 4.

When you find the queen, despatch her, then return all frames to one box, add the lid and move the

brood box back to the original location to reassemble the hive and add the new queen.

At the original location, remove the lid, use some smoke as these are the feisty foraging bees and place

the honey super on the up turned lid.

Return the brood box to the original location and add the new queen. Remember to cut the appropriate

end of the cage so the candy plug is exposed and facing upwards. Place between frames towards the

middle and ensure it won't slip.

Add your queen excluder, honey super/s and lid.

Monitor for acceptance. Wait at least 5-7 days before checking the new queen has been released from

the cage and is laying eggs.

What happens if you did not find the queen?

One method is to spread a sheet in front of the hive up to the hive entrance before reuniting the brood box

and super. Shake all bees from the brood box onto the sheet.

Place a queen excluder on top of the hive base, then add the brood box with brood frames. The bees will

walk back into the hive to look after the brood. Scan for the queen on the sheet, but if you still can't find her,

she will follow the bees back into the hive. The workers will be able to move up through the queen excluder

back to the frames, but the queen (and drones) will be trapped between the base and queen excluder. Leave

the bees for an hour or so or until they have all returned to the box before removing the brood box and

queen excluder to check the base for the queen. Hopefully now she will be easy to see! Despatch her, add

the new queen and reassemble the hive.
