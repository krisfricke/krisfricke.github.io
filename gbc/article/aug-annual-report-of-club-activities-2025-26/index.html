Annual Report of Club Activities 2025/26

Nathan Whitford · Pages 5–7 · August 2026

Dear Members,

Reflecting on the past year, it is remarkable to see just how much our club has achieved. It has been a year defined by growth, learning, community engagement and preparation. Beekeeping is never predictable, yet one constant has stood out: the willingness of our members to contribute their time, knowledge and enthusiasm to strengthen this club.

As expected, the spread of Varroa mite continues to be the most significant challenge facing Australian beekeeping. Throughout the year, our club placed strong emphasis on education and preparedness. We hosted presentations, discussions and practical demonstrations to help members understand what lies ahead and how best to manage it. A major highlight was successfully advocating for a second National Varroa Management Training course to be held in Geelong in July 2025. Despite competing with a Geelong Football Club home game, around 70 people attended. The turnout reinforced how important this issue is to local beekeepers and demonstrated the appetite for practical, evidence‑based training in our region.

I remain deeply impressed by the work of our Varroa Sub‑Committee. Bruce Ward, John Bemelmans, Jurg Schutz, Sue Moritz, Susan and Stephen Fridey, Stephen Davey, Ian Ashman, Stan and Deborah Filippis, John Edmonds and Helmut Woerner have devoted countless hours to preparing our members for the challenges ahead. Their ongoing development of practical Varroa management modules will be invaluable for years to come.

Education continued to be one of the club’s greatest strengths in additional to hive openings, practical demonstrations and monthly biosecurity sessions.We delivered four Beekeeping Essentials Courses across the past year August and October 2025, followed by February and March 2026, each one fully booked and supported by a dedicated team of volunteers.

Our guest speakers brought a wide range of knowledge and perspectives:

Stephen Fridey – Preparing for Varroa and spring management (July 2025)

Kris Fricke – Insights from The Australasian Beekeeper and his visit to Ghana (July 2025)

Cormac Farrell – Urban beekeeping and rooftop apiaries (Sept 2025)

Bruce Ward – American Foulbrood

Kris Fricke & Stan Filippis – Apimondia overview (Oct 2025)

Bruce Ward – AFB (Oct 2025)

Dr Kenya Fernandes – Healthy forests, healthy bees and active honey (Nov 2025)

Carmel Gerdesen – Bee Bingo (Dec 2025)

Lindsay Callaway – Honey Fighting Fund update (Feb 2026)

Adam Maxwell – National Bee Pest Surveillance Program (April 2026)

Dr Scarlett Howard – Bee intelligence, cognition and pollination (May 2026)

Sarsha Manuel – Black Beech Honey (June 2026)

While we are fortunate to attract outstanding guest speakers, we should never underestimate the value of the knowledge within our own membership. Many members stood at the front of the room this year to share their experiences, successes, failures and lessons learned. These presentations are often just as valuable as those delivered by industry experts. I also want to acknowledge the volunteers; Helmut Woerner, Scott Denno, Sue Moritz, Stephen Fridey, John Bemelmans and Bruce Ward, who continue to support our monthly Ask a Beekeeper sessions. Their willingness to answer questions, mentor newer members and share practical knowledge is a cornerstone of what makes this club special.

Community engagement remained one of the most rewarding aspects of our year. Committee members and volunteers represented the club at:

Royal Geelong Show (Oct 2025)

Le Festival Français (Oct 2025)

Ceres Makers Market (Nov 2025)

Bunnings events (Torquay, Sept 2025 & Waurn Ponds, July 2026)

The Heights Heritage House & Garden Autumn Fair (3 May 2026)

These events help raise awareness of beekeeping and allow us to connect directly with the public, promoting the importance of bees and pollinators.

In September 2025, most of our committee travelled to the Ballarat Regional Beekeepers Club to hear Mike Allerton speak about Varroa. It was a pleasure to visit another club that shares our passion and to strengthen ties between our organisations.

Our volunteers also continued engaging with community groups across the region, delivering presentations to:

Bellarine Retirement Village (Sept 2025)

Torquay Zeally Bay Probus Club

Geelong Day VIEW Club (Feb 2026)

Portarlington Probus Club ( May 2026)

Belmont Grange Nursing Home (May 2026)

Social Support Group of Hesse Rural Health (May 2026)

The club has also prepared a suite of presentation materials to support members who wish to engage with community groups and speak on behalf of the Geelong Beekeepers Club. These resources ensure our volunteers feel confident, well‑equipped and consistent in the information they share, helping us maintain a clear, evidence‑based message wherever we are represented. World Bee Day activities again generated strong interest, reminding us that fascination with bees extends well beyond our membership.

Media engagement increased this past 12 months, with both Stephen Fridey and myself appearing on Pulse FM’s Local Legends program. These interviews provided excellent opportunities to promote the club and highlight the importance of bees. We were also fortunate to secure five weeks of community service announcements across 3GL, K‑Rock and Bay FM, aired three times daily. Geelong Broadcasters generously extended this with an additional month of airtime on Bay FM in July. This level of exposure helped introduce the club to thousands of people across the region.

A significant milestone this past year was awarding Life Membership to Helmut Woerner atrthe August 2025 meeting. His contributions to education, mentoring, technical support and community outreach have had a lasting impact on the club. Whether building observation hives, assisting at workshops or helping new beekeepers find their feet, Helmut has consistently given his time and expertise for the benefit of others. The recognition was thoroughly deserved.

Our club apiary also saw its share of challenges. Due to roofing works at The Heights, our hives spent part of the year at Batesford under the care of Stephen and Susan Fridey before returning home. Thanks to the efforts of several committee members, the hives now sit on improved foundations with better access and working space.

Our infrastructure and resources continued to grow. We secured a City of Greater Geelong 2025– 26 Community Equipment Grant ($1,445.41), enabling the purchase of a new Flow Hive for training and demonstration. We expanded our library, improved website content, increased digital resources and continued investing in educational materials such as Hello Honey Bee flyer.

This past year also saw the introduction of paid advertising opportunities in our newsletter, with support from Bec’s Beehives and Aluen CAP. I would also like to thank John Edmonds (Edmonds Honey) and Stan Filippis (Stan’s Maleebee Farm) for their ongoing support to the club, their generosity plays an important role in the club’s success.

None of these achievements happen without volunteers. Behind every course, speaker event, hive opening, display stand, newsletter article, website update and committee meeting is a member giving their time to help others. The strength of our club has never been measured by the number of hives we collectively manage, it has always been the people who are willing to share knowledge, lend a hand and contribute to something larger than themselves.

As we look ahead, the challenges facing beekeeping remain significant. Varroa will continue to test us all. But if the past year has shown anything, it is that our club is well positioned to meet those challenges through education, collaboration and community.

Thank you to every committee member, volunteer, presenter, mentor and member who contributed throughout the year. Your efforts continue to make the Geelong Beekeepers Club one of the strongest and most active beekeeping clubs in Victoria.

I am proud of what we have achieved together and excited for what lies ahead. And yes, rumours suggest that live streaming of our club meetings may not be far away in fact I watched the July meeting remotely to help test the technologies.

Nathan Whitford

President, Geelong Beekeepers Club Inc.
