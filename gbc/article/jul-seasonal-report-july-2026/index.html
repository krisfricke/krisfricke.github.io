Seasonal Report, July 2026

John Edmonds · Pages 9–11 · July 2026

The weather has been erratic winter weather. Fortunately, some patchy rain has fallen to germinate the Canola crops. Locally the Geelong area has been dry, but areas in the South West and North West have enjoyed almost too much rain and have excellent breeding conditions at present. Around Mildura since March 13 inches of rain has fallen and resulted in the best breeding conditions ever seen. With Wards Weed, Wild Mustard and Turnip knee high. Today 17/6/2026 was 22 degrees which is 6 degrees above the average. The nights have been chilly.

Twin Leaf has started flowering and creates excellent breeding conditions. Barrel Clover will be good this spring and is eagerly sought after by the bees. I expect swarming could be a problem especially during almond pollination.

My friend took his hives to the Desert Banksia but unfortunately, they are not doing very much. I guess the dry autumn may be the problem. South Australian beekeepers report the same problem.

A Queensland beekeeper that has his bee hives on the Napunyah in outback Queensland channel country has a good honey flow and full bees but cannot get to them due to flooding. With more rain coming and almond pollination looming it may be having to shift the bees by helicopter at $1400 per hour cost.

Due to the weather being warmer than usual in Queensland. Eucalyptus trees that normally flower in summer have commenced flowering. The Yellow Box tree opposite my property has commenced flowering, hopefully others do not follow suit.

Some areas in the Mallee region have Canola crops coming into flower. Locally it is still only small plants that should flower beginning of August. I see the canola is very patchy in its germination this year, possibly the sowing into dry soil has been a problem. Eastern Grey Kangaroos are grazing the young plants and seem to be increasing their numbers rapidly.

I was unable to attend the VAA conference but my friend reported that the speech by Steve Martin a British varroa researcher gave us optimism for the future. Fortunately, so far, the Australian varroa does not have “Deformed Wing Virus” this is the bee killer. Without this virus hives should be able to contend with up to 3,000 varroa mites in a hive. If we select the best preforming bee hives to breed from, it is possible that varroa resistant bees will be possible. This could be achieved in five years. I find this interesting as a commercial beekeeper in Victoria has not treated his bees or even looked for varroa despite being in an area infected by varroa. He has more than 80 tonnes of honey harvested from his hives this season. It will be interesting to see how the hives survive the winter.

Queensland beekeepers have had their bee hives in the Channel country and gained a crop of Napunyah honey. They purchased Faba Bean Flour from me as the available pollen is very poor. The bees worked open drums on their sides of Faba Bean flour and Ultra Bee supplement side by side and the bees gathered both feeds slightly favoring the Faba Bean Flour. I fed my hives patties made with Faba Bean flour, sugar, Soy flour and Agrisea. Only scraps of the patties are left in the hives. It is important to place the patties directly over the brood frames in the center of the brood nest before the bees shut down for the season. When the weather becomes cold the bees lose interest in consuming the patties.

Almond pollination prices seem to vary greatly from $170 to $250 per hive. It is predicted that 80,000 hives are short for this season.

Tocal college are going to conduct Chemical User courses as beekeepers using chemical varroa treatments need to have completed this course.

Honey prices have increased at the honey packers.

Honey flows for next season look scarce in Queensland and New South Wales for the forthcoming season I am told. The floral sources seem to be coming into flower slightly earlier than usual.

Spanish Heath a noxious weed of wet areas is flowering and the bees are strong and making brood.

Sallow Wattle has its first flowers at Aireys Inlet but only budded around Geelong. Mount Morgan wattle has the first flowers opening.

Yellow Gum is flowering but will most likely finish flowering before the warm spring weather. Red Box has an excellent budding. Messmate appears to be very patchy with some areas looking good but many other areas not budded.

Canola crops vary from just small seedlings out of the ground to nice small plants locally.

Farmers have been spraying Canola crops for Earwigs. I had to shift bees due to spraying. Caterpillars abound in the Mallee and it could possibly be a Bogong moth season. They walk over the flowers at night and leave a scent that repels the bees.

Mice could become a problem as their numbers increase. Reduced entrances help keeping them out of the hive.

The following snippets have been taken from the March 2026 Beekeepers Quarterly

Yellow-legged Asian hornets are spreading in England. In USA Georgia and South Carolina have been difficult to eradicate due to marshy and Savanah areas.

USA colony losses of over 60% of commercial bee hives in 2025. High levels of acute paralysis virus and deformed wing virus were found along with resistance to Amitraz (Apivar) which are thought to be the problems.

European beekeepers staged a conference to understand the biology of varroa resistance. Examples of Treatment free beekeeping were given by Kirk Webster from Vermont USA and the wife Anita Reinsertsen whose late husband kept 400 beehives for over 25 years treatment free. Discussions regarding breeding and selection for varroa resistance and open mated locally adapted queens. More conferences will be held in 2026 in Poland, Germany, and Austria.

Bees prefer to eat freshly stored pollen. Bee bread is produced by a two-stage process of firstly Lactobacillus Kunkeei which metabolizes the fructose sugars in the mixed pollen and nectar. The second stage involves fermentation using the yeast Saccharomyces. The yeasts help preserve the bee bread. Nurse bees convert the bee bread into brood food. Exposure to crop spraying fungicides will destroy yeast cells and allowing the bee bread to spoil. Formic acid used to control varroa can disrupt the PH conditions for some yeast and bacterial species.

A commercial beekeeper made an interesting comment that when bees cap over pollen it most likely is contaminated by fungicides.???? I thought they put a cap of honey over it turn it into bee bread and then capped it for future use.

I see wattles forming buds, and at Torquay the Cape Wattle has been flowering for a few weeks. This wattle is very weedy but is odd in that it yields nectar and pollen.

Protein patties need to be placed on top of the brood frames in late autumn before the weather gets cold. If a cold snap occurs like this season the bees ignore it as they reduce brood nests. Timing is very important.

The Varroa Heat Map is a waste of time as I suspect beekeepers are not reporting or testing their bee hives for varroa. Sales of varroa treatment have been made to beekeepers that are in areas not showing varroa. Infected beekeepers are shifting their hives to bee sites that are not shown on the map.

Melbourne Polytechnique 13 beekeepers and 2 teachers visited Edmonds Honey at the completion of their cert 3 course. I explained my ways of beehive operation and nucleus bee hives for queen bee production. Varroa control was discussed and screen bottom board design. Fortunately, the weather was pleasant for the day. Millena showed the shops beekeeping stock and answered questions about the various beekeeping tools etc. After lunch a quick look of the workshop, Honey extraction and Honey packing sheds. Just not enough time for everything.

I have been watching a YouTube video of Oxalic Acid dribble method from America. It recommended using Oxalic Acid from the hardware store that is used for bleaching timber. Also, I spoke to a New South Wales beekeeper that had also been doing the same. Apparently, the purity of the Oxalic Acid used for bleaching timber is not food grade and may contain heavy metals and poison. Your honey will be contaminated.

My friend sent me the results of his washing the bees for varroa. It seems the Oxalic Acid strips have worked well and low levels of varroa were detected. The varroa levels have not increased and are still in the 1 to 4 range. But a hive that missed the mid-March treatment had a 28 varroa reading from a quick wash, (14/06/2026) most likely many more mites are possibly in the hive with a through wash. An inspection of the brood revealed it was a bit patchy but the hive had still made a box of honey. Further inspections in the spring will be interesting, hopefully the hive will not collapse.

I have made copies of the Randy Oliver wash bottles using clear plastic drinking cups. The holes I made are slightly larger than those in the commercial models. At the bee site I wash the bees, and number the hives and wash bottles and take them home to strain and record the varroa numbers. The bumpy roads improve the wash results as a reading of 6 mites at the apiary becomes 9 when the liquid is strained at home. If you use windscreen wiper fluid in your wash the varroa are not killed by the solution and are still alive when you strain it.

A beekeeper detected varroa in his hives and purchased Aluen Cap treatments. The bees had been in the edge of the Otways in the autumn, where they possibly picked up the varroa. An exbeekeeper located half way between Lorne and Winchelsea found a bee in the house and while removing it discovered 2 varroa mites attached to the bee. No reports of varroa are listed on the heat map nearby.

I have two test hives near Anglesea. Commercial beekeepers from Bendigo have been in the area and we now have varroa. Mid-March the first reports of varroa were being found in recreational beekeepers hives located close to the beekeeper. After 3 months I washed the two-test hives today 17/6/2026 and found 65 varroa in the hive with 6 frames of brood, the second hive has 2 frames of brood and is full of honey with 36 varroa mites. Hopefully the Oxalic Acid strips will reduce the varroa numbers. The hives are strong doubles full with bees and drone brood.

I placed hives in a cold area with winter Ironbark in flower. The hives are full and need to be extracted. I hoped there would be no brood for the varroa to breed on. And will be OK until being shifted to early Canola flowering. A couple of hives showed varroa as one mite in a wash. Today 17/6/2026 a few more appear to have varroa only in ones and twos but still many appear to be still clean. My expectations of the hives being out of brood were misguided as they had 2 or more frames of brood. The hive scales show they gain weight on the warm days and lose it on the cold and windy days, but have remained a consistent weight ranging from 45 to 65 kgs.
