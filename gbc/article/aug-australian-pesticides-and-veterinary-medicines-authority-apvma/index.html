Australian Pesticides and Veterinary Medicines Authority (APVMA)

Nathan Whitford · Page 21 · August 2026

Following on from my July President's Report regarding access to Varroa treatment products, I have now received a response from the Australian Pesticides and Veterinary Medicines Authority (APVMA).

The APVMA has acknowledged the practical and compliance challenges that arise when available treatment pack sizes do not align with the needs of smaller-scale beekeepers.

In response to our concerns, the APVMA stated that it "appreciates that this may result in increased product wastage and create difficulties for beekeepers seeking to comply with the permit conditions." However, it also noted that "the APVMA can only assess and approve the information put before us in an application. The pack size and associated label instructions under PER95790 were proposed by the permit holder as part of their application. The APVMA cannot require a permit holder to market or supply alternative pack sizes."

The APVMA further advised that "the most effective way to pursue a change is for beekeepers and industry groups to raise the issue directly with the permit holder. If the permit holder elects to seek approval for additional or smaller pack sizes, they may submit an application to the APVMA, and we would assess that application on its merits."

The irony in all of this is that Aluen CAP sales representatives are very keen to attend bee club meetings and promote their product. It is a product that many of our members would like access to, but few hobbyist beekeepers need a 60-treatment pack. The current packaging presents an unnecessary barrier for smaller operators who are trying to manage Varroa responsibly while remaining compliant with permit requirements.

I strongly encourage beekeepers to contact their local State and Federal Members of Parliament to highlight the value of hobbyist beekeeping and the significant impact that Varroa is having on their beekeeping operations. Policymakers need to understand that practical, affordable access to treatment options is essential if we are to support healthy bee populations and a sustainable beekeeping sector. As a club we will continue to provide feedback to Aluen CAP regarding the pack size.

Nathan Whitford - President.
