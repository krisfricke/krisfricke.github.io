NW Zone Varroa Update

Ian Ashman · Page 22 · September 2026

As NW Zone members undertook their first full inspections of spring, varroa monitoring at Inverleigh apiaries during late August revealed varied results, with mite levels remaining low overall. Soapy water wash testing showed positive progress: three hives recorded zero mites (including one previously identified as positive in April), three hives had one mite each, and one stronger hive had three mites. The important takeaway is that varroa prevalence appears patchy across the zone. Interestingly, drone uncapping emerged as an effective screening tool for early detection, sometimes revealing mites before standard alcohol or soapy water washes can detect them. Members were reminded that treatment is not yet required for these apiaries, with most still having at least one to two months before intervention becomes necessary. Spring inspections revealed strong hive conditions overall, with most apiaries showing solid brood patterns, abundant pollen reserves, and excellent bee activity. Beekeepers reported significant pollen collection from local wattle and canola sources, indicating good forage availability. Several practical techniques were discussed, including regular hive weight monitoring as a reliable method for assessing winter survival and spring build-up without opening the hive excessively. With swarming season approaching, beekeepers were encouraged to remain vigilant about prevention strategies and to share any questions or swarm management experiences with the wider zone.
