This month's bit of bee biology: Adult worker body parts

Bruce Ward · Page 12 · July 2026

(Article 26 by Bruce Ward)

In the last article, we looked at the anatomy of the honey bee larva and pupa. This article looks at the overall form of the adult worker.

Body segments of the worker larva compared to the body segments of the adult worker. (Image courtesy of Adam Tofilski at https://honeybee.drawwing.org).

You can see from the above drawing that the larva looks nothing like the adult worker bee that it becomes, but if you look closely the segments remain largely consistent.

The head alters shape and sprouts eyes, antennae and enhanced mouthparts.

The next three larval segments, labelled T1 to T3 become the thorax. Each segment of the thorax has sprouted a pair of legs, and T2 and T3 have sprouted a pair of wings as well.

The next seven segments, labelled A1 to A7 have become the abdomen that we see on the adult bee. The first abdominal segment looks like part of the thorax, but it does not have any legs or wings attached, so by definition is part of the abdomen and it includes the waist!

So what happened to A8, A9 and A10? They are still there, hidden away inside the abdomen, and greatly modified. They have become the sting and its related structures!

Special thanks to Adam Tofilski for the excellent diagrams used in this series. You can see more of Adam’s amazing drawings of bees at https://honeybee.drawwing.org
