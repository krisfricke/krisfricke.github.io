Seasonal Report, September 2026

John Edmonds · Pages 10–12 · September 2026

Despite the warnings of a dry and hotter spring, so far it is the wettest conditions for many years. Farmers crops are looking good and more showers seem to keep coming. Unfortunately, as of the start of September the Canola is flowering and breeding strong hives but no weight gain is reported by the hive scales. Over winter weights in most areas were only 1 kilogram less than the autumn weight. Strangely it seems the city hives did not do as well as the rural hives.

Excellent flowering is expected from Capeweed, Clovers, Bottlebrushes, Coastal Teatree, Coastal Bearded Heath and Yellow Gum.

Reports that hives around Point Lonsdale and Anglesea are full of honey. A customer reported catching the first swarm of the season at Point Lonsdale.

The Almond pollination flowering is finishing and hives are to be removed by the 10/9/2026 as spraying fungicide will occur. Many bee hives from NSW and Queensland did not arrive due to the varroa mite weakening the hive strength, or wet conditions causing trucks to be bogged. Some beekeepers had large loss of bee hives over winter. Unfortunately, many Victorian bee hives will have become infected with the "Super Mite" from Queensland. Ultimately it will spread to Victorian bee hives.

Oxalic acid works extremely well at controlling Varroa in bee hives. The Varroa have sticky wet suckers to grip the bees. The Oxalic acid burns their feet damaging the pads so they fall off and die. The bees are dry so not affected by the Oxalic acid. It can be dispensed by vaporization of the crystals using a heated vaporizer at 265 degrees. Unfortunately, this requires wearing the correct mask with filters, eye protection, gloves and disposable suit. Only half of the Oxalic acid actually is effective and the nozzle delivering the vapour may become blocked. This procedure is best done when all the bees are in the beehive. The Oxalic acid can be dribbled as a liquid over the bees in between the frames of the brood nest and the bees clean themselves and remove the mites. This is done best with vegetable glycerin rather than sugar syrup as the agent to distribute the Oxalic acid. The easiest way is to use kraft paper strips that have been soaked in the Oxalic acid and vegetable glycerin mixture. I recommend purchasing the strips as the temperature control is critical during the dissolving the Oxalic acid otherwise Formic acid can be easily produced which is far stronger and can cause bee and queen deaths. It is essential that 99% food grade Oxalic acid is used as lesser quality Oxalic acid is sold for cleaning timber and rust removal. Oxalic acid only kills the Varroa mites that are on the bees and not the mites under the capping. So, the strips last at least 8 weeks and continually kill the mites on hatching bees. It is a safe product that does not damage the honey and beeswax. Many overseas countries like Argentina where the Aluen Cap comes from have used it for 40 years with strips continually in the bee hive and produce large amounts of honey.

YouTube has many video clips showing it being made and used but I caution beekeepers as many videos are not using it safely. Baking soda neutralizes the acid and should be used for washing all surfaces and kept on hand when handling the strips to wash gloves etc.

Formic acid is stronger as it will kill mites under the capping but is cruel as it burns the wounds caused by the developing Varroa in the cells and these bees cluster outside the bee hive and many die.

Varroa is showing up around Geelong. Reports and customers purchasing Oxalic Acid strips from Inverleigh, Teesdale, Winchelsea, Moriac, Forrest, Anglesea, Hamilton, Little River, Werribee, Werribee South, Corio, and Balliang.

One can conclude that nowhere is safe from Varroa now. Especially as swarming occurs. Beekeepers catching and shifting swarms to new areas will transfer the varroa.

Customers are purchasing the Oxalic Acid varroa treatment strips. I think this is a safe method as it does not contaminate the honey or beeswax. You must wear nitrile gloves as the acid will soak into leather gloves and possibly burn your hands. Do not have it come into contact with your eyes. I have been told of New South Wales beekeepers that have used the strips back-to-back for three years successfully. I do not like the Vaping of Oxalic Acid due to the possibility of accidental damage to the beekeepers lungs and eyes and forming kidney stones. Cheap imported vaporizers off the internet may block up the nozzle quickly and run out of battery power after only a few hives. Also, it seems that the procedure needs to be done every six days and often the Varroa numbers remain high. A friend vaped his hives at Inverleigh and the dead mites on the sticky mat were too many to count, described as "thousands".

I have been uncapping drone brood as it is quicker than washes and the varroa stand out on the white pupae and can be easily seen, often walking around. So far, I have found two hives that had large numbers of Varroa despite other bee hives in the apiary having only low counts. I guess that these bee hives have robbed out dying bee hives and been reinfected. Bee hives located at Ocean Grove, Grovedale and You Yangs have so far been free of Varroa.

It seems the drones are spreading the Varroa to other bee hives and especially those that have had virgin queens in them and have attracted the drones. I have never seen in books where any comments have been made about this.

An apiary located between Teesdale and Inverleigh had six bee hives with Varroa in the drone brood and thirty bee hives that were still clean. It was interesting to note the infected bee hives faced to the west or were on the corners of the apiary. Perhaps the drones flew in from the west.

I think removal of frames containing drone comb will be important (30% drop) and screen bottoms (20% drop). I have seen an interesting idea to make a screen rim that fits on top of the existing solid bottom board and allows a corflute board coated with vegetable oil to slide in to monitor the varroa drop.

I am inspecting hives and recording the mite counts. Strangely I have found hives that had mites in washes in May 2026 but now I cannot find mites in the drone brood. Unless the hive is heavily Varroa infested it is unlikely you will find Varroa in worker brood.

Experts say that the first 2 to 3 years need constant vigilance, but after then the feral and unmanaged hives will have died and honey flows are much greater. Three treatments per year are predicted to be sufficient.

Varroa has been in overseas countries for 40 years The European Union, Argentina, and United Kingdom and Turkey yet they make significant honey yields each year. We just have to learn how to deal with it.
